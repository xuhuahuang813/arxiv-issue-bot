### 1. ProvCards: Artifact-Oriented Provenance for Heterogeneous Scientific Workflows

- arXiv ID: `2611.24894v1`
- Authors: Tanja Auge, Marinus Holitschke, Meike Klettke
- Published: 2026-11-18
- Categories: `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24894v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24894v1)

Reproducibility in scientific workflows is hindered by heterogeneous components and execution environments, as well as fragmented provenance capture. Although existing approaches offer established semantics and workflow-specific solutions, provenance remains difficult to capture, exchange, and compose across workflow boundaries. We present ProvCards, an artifact-oriented approach that treats provenance as a first-class artifact grounded in the W3C PROV Data Model. Individual workflow components can generate cards that are stored, exchanged, reused, and composed independently of any workflow system or centralized execution context. ProvDecks group related cards into portable scopes while preserving their identities. ProvGraphs provide derived views for provenance reconstruction, querying, visualization, and analysis. Together, these representations support coherent, task-specific provenance views across heterogeneous components, facilitating traceability, reuse, and reproducibility. We implement ProvCards using a graph-based infrastructure and evaluate them in a scientific data-processing workflow. The results demonstrate independent provenance capture at the component level, composition across component boundaries, and reconstruction for querying and analysis. ProvCards provide a portable, composable, and incrementally extensible foundation for provenance management in heterogeneous scientific workflows.

### 2. Towards Online Adaptive LLM-based Semantic Filtering

- arXiv ID: `2611.24518v1`
- Authors: Ziqi Zou, Zhenyan Nie, Kangfei Zhao, Zhiwei Zhang, Aoqian Zhang, Peng Wu, Xuefeng Zhao
- Published: 2026-11-18
- Categories: `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24518v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24518v1)

Large language models (LLMs) enable semantic filtering over unstructured documents using natural language predicates, but invoking an oracle LLM on every document incurs prohibitive monetary cost and online latency. Existing model-cascade approaches reduce this cost by using a lightweight proxy model to score each document and forwarding only uncertain documents to the oracle. However, their proxy models have limited generalization capability, and they typically adopt fixed sampling budgets for proxy training and threshold calibration, which insufficiently adapts to diverse online queries. To address this, we present OASIS, an online adaptive framework for LLM-based semantic filtering. OASIS combines a query-agnostic document similarity prior with query-adaptive online learning and sampling. In the offline phase, OASIS utilizes query-agnostic document similarity as a prior and constructs a document navigating spread-out graph (NSG) over document embeddings. For each online query, OASIS trains a query-conditioned graph attention network over the document NSG to obtain proxy scores through semi-supervised node classification. To allocate labels effectively, OASIS adopts density-aware active learning to explore uncertain score regions, selectively annotate informative documents, retrain the proxy, and stop early when additional labels are unlikely to improve the cascade. During threshold calibration, OASIS progressively samples an adaptively sized calibration set, adapting calibration cost to the query's score distribution. Extensive experiments demonstrate that OASIS achieves a 1.75x-5.42x reduction in online latency and reduces the number of LLM-processed documents by 35%-75% relative to the state-of-the-art methods at an achieved accuracy of 0.9.

### 3. Cascadeability and the Realization Gap: An Empirical Study of LLM-Powered Semantic Filtering

- arXiv ID: `2611.23898v1`
- Authors: Nan Hou, Kangfei Zhao, Jiadong Xie, Jeffrey Xu Yu
- Published: 2026-11-18
- Categories: `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23898v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23898v1)

Semantic filters enable natural language predicates over unstructured documents, but evaluating every document with a large language model (LLM) oracle is expensive. Proxy cascades reduce oracle invocations using cheaper surrogates, but oracle reduction alone cannot distinguish proxy limitations from calibration bottlenecks and establish practical efficiency. We present a unified benchmark of four representative systems, which formulate query processing as a three-stage workflow comprising proxy construction, threshold calibration, and cascade inference, using a two-threshold acceptance rule. We introduce cascadeability: the maximum empirical oracle reduction attainable from fixed proxy scores at a specified accuracy target, computed retrospectively using complete oracle labels. We investigate 8 research questions, spanning proxy quality, end-to-end performance, workload sensitivities, and component ablations, and reveal our key findings accordingly. Comparing cascadeability with achieved oracle reduction yields a realization gap that, interpreted alongside accuracy, exposes distinct proxy and calibration bottlenecks. The benchmark and diagnostics support evaluation beyond oracle reduction and motivate joint proxy-calibration design, class-sensitive quality assessment, and end-to-end cost optimization. We release a dataset-query benchmark, containing 10 real-world workloads across various domains and difficulties, with annotations of two LLM families.

### 4. Logical Design Tuning via Workload-aware E/R-to-Relational Mapping

- arXiv ID: `2611.23632v1`
- Authors: Hasara Kalumin, Amol Deshpande
- Published: 2026-11-17
- Categories: `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23632v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23632v1)

Conceptual data models, like the E/R model, can provide a more stable and intuitive user-facing interface and better logical independence for applications, compared to the relational model which is forced to closely mirror the physical representation. However, this flexibility introduces a challenge as well as an opportunity: a single conceptual schema admits many semantically equivalent relational schemas, whose performance can differ substantially across workloads. Traditional mapping methods use a set of predefined transformation rules or designer-selected mapping strategies, rather than optimizing the relational realization with respect to the expected workload. In this paper, we present a cost-based framework for workload-aware conceptual-to-relational mapping. Given an E/R schema and a representative workload, our framework automatically selects an efficient relational schema by optimizing mapping decisions, including inheritance, entity and relationship representations. User-specified E/R queries are compiled into SQL over the selected relational realization, allowing existing relational query optimizers to determine the physical execution plan. Experiments on real-world-inspired schemas show that mapping choices strongly affect performance; fixed or heuristic strategies exhibit large variability across workloads, whereas our approach consistently achieves competitive performance avoiding pathological cases.

### 5. The Value Semantics Layer: Enabling Machine-Understandable Databases for Agentic AI

- arXiv ID: `2611.23629v1`
- Authors: Jalal Mahmud, Eser Kandogan
- Published: 2026-11-17
- Categories: `cs.DB`, `cs.AI`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23629v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23629v1)

As data systems increasingly support autonomous LLM-powered agents alongside human analysts, they require richer semantic infrastructure bridging analytical concepts and analytical operations. Existing metadata systems expose schemas, catalogs, lineage, and governance information, but provide limited support for representing operational semantics. We propose the Value Semantics Layer (VSL), an architectural abstraction that externalizes value semantics as persistent, planner-visible infrastructure. VSL introduces Value Semantic Types (VSTs), reusable operational semantic assertions capturing distributional, relational, process, quantitative, and policy-grounded semantics. Unlike descriptive metadata, VSTs enable planners to validate analytical operations, select appropriate operators, and guide analytical workflows. We present the motivation, formalization, architecture, and prototype realizations of VSL, and outline a research agenda toward AI-native semantic infrastructure for analytical systems.

### 6. Exploring multimodal AI--XR interactions for software development: From expert perspectives to semantic code outlines

- arXiv ID: `2611.24334v1`
- Authors: Ilya Zakharov, Alexey Markin, Konstantin Ostroukhov, Alexey Perminov, Roham Koohestani, Ekaterina Koshchenko, Agnia Sergeyuk
- Published: 2026-11-18
- Categories: `cs.HC`, `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24334v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24334v1)

AI tools can generate code quickly, but developers still need to understand and check the results. We explore how the enriched multimodal context derived from the extended reality (XR) workspace might help developers manage this information. We first interviewed 13 experts in AI, XR, and related fields to identify opportunities and design challenges (Study 1). These interviews informed two prototypes explored with 12 professional developers (Study 2): four tried a version where the code faded to become invisible after it was looked at, and eight tried a version that replaced viewed code sections with short descriptions of their purpose. In the second prototype, head direction helped determine when to offer a change, while manual input let developers apply it and reopen the original code. A separate open-ended demonstration with seven software-engineering researchers provided complementary feedback. Participants' accounts suggested that descriptions could provide an overview of unfamiliar code and serve as reminders when revisiting it. However, difficulty comparing descriptions with source code, headset limitations, and controls that deviates from regular IDE routines could introduce additional effort. These exploratory findings identify requirements for attention support: make summaries easy to recognize and access, preserve straightforward inspection of the original code, and retain familiar navigation and editing practices. The study contributes to human-centered multimodal systems by showing how the coordination of input channels and AI-generated representations must account for users' attention, verification needs, and established ways of working.

### 7. Inference-Time Scaling in Agentic Hardware Verification Workflows: A Multi-Strategy Characterization Across Protocol Complexity

- arXiv ID: `2611.23905v1`
- Authors: Vikash Kumar
- Published: 2026-11-18
- Categories: `cs.AR`, `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23905v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23905v1)

Inference-time scaling, allocating more compute during generation rather than pre-training, has demonstrated dramatic quality improvements for LLM reasoning tasks. Its behaviour in structured hardware verification code generation, where outputs must satisfy compiler correctness, protocol semantics, and functional coverage constraints simultaneously, is less understood. We present a systematic, simulator-grounded study of inference-time compute scaling for agentic UVM testbench synthesis targeting AMBA protocol bridges. Four inference strategies---zero-shot (\$0.04), raw-feedback iterative (\$0.81), structured inner-loop (\$0.95), and full dual-loop agentic synthesis (\$1.06)---are evaluated across four AMBA targets (Protocol Complexity Index 11.8--22.6) using the Protocol Verification Completeness (PVC) metric, functional coverage, and a six-class UVM error taxonomy (E1--E6). Our central finding is that structured diagnostic feedback acts as an inference-time compute multiplier: the structured inner loop yields roughly $15\times$ more PVC per inference dollar than raw EDA-tool feedback (marginal efficiency, S1$\to$S2 vs. S0$\to$S1). The mechanism is fault targeting, not token reduction: raw compiler feedback drives the testbench to compile but cannot surface functional protocol faults that leave the code syntactically valid, whereas structured E1--E6 bundles diagnose and resolve them. All outputs are judged by a cycle-accurate Mentor Questa toolchain rather than by model self-assessment; the dual-loop strategy reaches PVC $\geq$ 0.95 with zero scoreboard errors on every target, and the generated environments surfaced a latent RTL bug in two of the four reference designs. These results establish a principled compute-quality trade-off curve for LLM-aided verification environment generation and motivate inference-budget-aware deployment strategies for industrial EDA workflows.

### 8. Validating Concurrent Correctness Witnesses with Ghosts by Thread-Modular Abstract Interpretation

- arXiv ID: `2611.23792v1`
- Authors: Michael Schwarz, Zhendong Ang, Umang Mathur
- Published: 2026-11-18
- Categories: `cs.PL`, `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23792v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23792v1)

Concurrent programs are notoriously difficult to write and to reason about, and even tools based on sound theoretical foundations can produce incorrect results due to implementation bugs. Correctness witnesses have been proposed as a way to increase the confidence in the verdicts of software verifiers by enabling independent validation. Recently, correctness witnesses have been extended to the concurrent setting, by introducing ghost variables to allow encoding more expressive invariants. While different approaches to producing witnesses with ghosts exist, validation techniques are still lacking; instead of leveraging the information encoded in the ghosts to guide the validation process, validators merely treat ghost instrumentation and invariants over ghost variables as additional proof obligations. We propose a novel framework for the validation of concurrent correctness witnesses that exploits the semantic information encoded in ghost variables. It combines a checking phase, which establishes the semantic content of the ghost instrumentation, with an exploitation phase making use of this information. Both phases are formulated as thread-modular abstract interpretation. In particular, we consider two different classes of ghost variables and techniques for exploiting them, one based on resource invariants and one based on phases. Phase ghosts guide the degree to which the abstract interpretation considers interleavings instead of always abstracting them coarsely. In this way, the abstract interpreter can validate witnesses containing invariants beyond the reach of unguided thread-modular analysis, while still retaining its scalability benefits. We have implemented our framework in a static analyzer and evaluate it on witnesses encoding resource invariants obtained from a state-of-the-art software verifier, as well as on hand-crafted witnesses encoding phase information.

### 9. RefinementGuard: Counterexample-Directed Semantic Qualification for Hidden Agent-Tool Execution

- arXiv ID: `2611.23611v1`
- Authors: Adhyayan Veer Singh, Fanjiang Ye, Yuke Wang
- Published: 2026-11-17
- Categories: `cs.AI`, `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23611v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23611v1)

Tool runtimes can lower an agent-selected operation into a hidden parallel graph, reducing latency and context while risking semantic mistranslation. We introduce RefinementGuard: translation validation for hidden, effectful agent-tool lowerings, coupled to artifact-bound, fail-closed runtime admission. Qualification compares a candidate graph with an independent reference on isolated state using explicit parent-operation obligations. We evaluate two repository-level contracts, bug localization and patch validation, on controlled repositories. Among 100 activated, non-equivalent development/qualification faults adjudicated by one human, parent-semantic validation rejects 49/100 (operator-cluster bootstrap 95% CI [33.7%, 64.8%]). The objective is selective admission, not maximum mutation rejection: exact equality rejects 100/100 faults but also all 60 valid variants, whereas obligation-based validation accepts 60/60; its 51 residual misses identify concrete specification gaps. On a frozen disjoint input split, the held-out B2-B3 result is localization-only because 21 patch mutants fail structurally; B3 rejects 38/56 localization defects. In a controlled asynchronous-delay regime, the localization serial/guarded median-latency ratio is 4.50x, and hidden execution exposes 74.3% less parent-visible context than visible execution. Patch validation shows no demonstrated speedup; zero-delay runs expose overhead. End-to-end qualification thus detects lower-layer misses while preserving localization's parallelism.

### 10. Record Sufficiency: What a Decision Record Must Preserve When an Unsettled Claim Becomes an Operative Status

- arXiv ID: `2611.23422v1`
- Authors: Amadeus Brandes
- Published: 2026-11-17
- Categories: `cs.SE`, `cs.CY`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23422v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23422v1)

Organisations keep evidence, logs and approvals, but often not the step by which an unsettled claim became an operative status, such as an accepted label or a released image, so later readers cannot tell what the status was allowed to mean. We call a decision record sufficient if it is true and a reader who has only the record and the items it cites can explain the status, bound it, re-derive it and challenge it. From these four uses we argue for ten questions a record must answer, map them onto the author's earlier JSON Schema for decision records (DRK v0.1), and define four mechanical checks. We separate conformance, which the checks decide; adequacy, whether the answers are specific enough to use, which needs a reader; fidelity, which no check on the record decides; and merits, which sufficiency does not require. On 164 single-defect variants of three synthetic records, schema validation, the only check DRK v0.1 implements, rejects every variant with a deleted required property and none of the others, and it accepts a record whose claim, rule, scope, limits and challenge route are empty. These are properties of the schema shown on defects we seeded, not estimates of performance on real records. A false record passes every check. The statement-of-reasons schema of the EU Digital Services Act Transparency Database answers 4 of the questions in full, 2 in part and 4 not at all: why the matter was unsettled, what the status depends on, whether it can be re-derived, and how to challenge it. In 25 real statements from 25 platforms, drawn by a seeded rule and coded by a language model and checked by the author, free text addressed any of those four in only 2, and in 18 (24 under a stricter reading adopted during coding) no free text said why the item fell under the rule. No deployment or legal claim is made.
