### 1. ARACDE: Retrofitting Databases with ABAC using External Authorization Engines

- arXiv ID: `2610.18660v1`
- Authors: Karthikeya S M Yelisetty, Shamik Sural, Santosh Nagarakatte
- Published: 2026-10-13
- Categories: `cs.CR`, `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18660v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18660v1)

This paper proposes a methodology for decoupling the authorization logic of a relational database from its core data management engine, enabling specification of advanced access control policies with minimal changes to the code base. Our decoupled authorization architecture, which we call ARCADE, externalizes access request resolution to a formally verifiable policy engine (e.g., Cedar), while the database itself enforces those decisions. We showcase the capabilities of ARCADE on two widely popular open-source databases, namely MySQL and PostgreSQL. Purpose built lightweight plugins add an Attribute-based Access Control (ABAC) layer that augments the native discretionary and role-based access control capabilities of these databases. ARCADE supports highly expressive policies using attributes of subjects, objects, and the environment, and the use of Cedar enables easy auditing and verification of the specified policies. It follows a design principle that mitigates potential risks in segregating authorization from the database core through low footprint, network isolation, and a policy composition methodology that does not negate any native authorization. Experiments on TPC-C, TPC-B, and a custom workload show that ARCADE incurs negligible performance overhead, for instance, less than 3.9% throughput penalty for MySQL (1.3% for PostgreSQL) on industry standard TPC-C benchmark.

### 2. RT-J: Large-Scale Pretraining of Relational Transformers for Context-Efficient Predictions

- arXiv ID: `2610.17867v1`
- Authors: Rishabh Ranjan, Vignesh Kothapalli, Harshvardhan Agarwal, Charilaos Kanatsoulis, Roshan Upendra, Tom Palczewski, Carlos Guestrin, Jure Leskovec
- Published: 2026-10-13
- Categories: `cs.LG`, `cs.AI`, `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17867v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.17867v1)

Foundation models for tables and relational databases are increasingly used for predictive tasks across various scientific and industrial domains. However, adapting existing models to unseen datasets requires tens of thousands of examples. Here we introduce RT-J, an 85M-parameter relational foundation model (RFM) that makes accurate predictions even with hundreds of in-context examples or fewer. To achieve this, we first assemble the Join, a large collection of 639 web-crawled databases with 6,023 LLM-generated forecasting tasks. We pretrain with long contexts of up to 8,192 database cells, using in-context examples from a novel lightweight random-walk retriever. Finally, we leverage context tuning and ensembling to scale up test-time compute. On in-context regression tasks from RelBench, RT-J requires 10x fewer examples on average to match strong feature engineering pipelines with LLM-agent or RDBLearn featurizers and TabICLv2 or LightGBM predictors. Overall, our work pushes the frontier for context-efficient predictions on relational data and highlights the importance of real-world semantics, context engineering and large-scale pretraining for RFMs.

### 3. ML Engineering Agents - A Data Management Perspective

- arXiv ID: `2610.16919v1`
- Authors: Arnab Phani, Elias Strauss, Tim Schuster, Sebastian Schelter
- Published: 2026-10-12
- Categories: `cs.DB`, `cs.LG`, `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16919v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.16919v1)

Machine learning engineering (MLE) agents are emerging as a new paradigm for automating end-to-end ML pipeline development. Using large language models for code generation and predictive performance as a machine-gradable feedback signal, these agents iteratively synthesize, execute, and refine pipeline code. Yet current systems are largely evaluated on pre-built, single-table benchmarks that abstract away key data management challenges. This tutorial introduces the foundations of MLE agents and highlights research opportunities for the database community. We cover how agents combine program synthesis with iterative and evolutionary optimization, and discuss open challenges in efficient pipeline execution, data discovery and augmentation over heterogeneous sources, reliability and debugging, as well as fairness and compliance. We connect these challenges to existing data management techniques and conclude with a hands-on session using contemporary MLE agents.

### 4. SemReuse: Certified Reuse of Cached Natural-Language Views for Semantic Operators

- arXiv ID: `2610.16035v1`
- Authors: Phat T. Tran-Truong, Tung Kieu, Xuan-Bach Le, Son X. Ha, Duc Kieu, Bin Yang, Christian S. Jensen
- Published: 2026-10-12
- Categories: `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16035v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.16035v1)

Semantic operators allow users to filter unstructured data using conditions expressed in natural language, but existing systems typically invoke an LLM for every input tuple. Analytical workloads often contain repeated or related filters whose results could be reused. Such reuse is risky because it requires inferring whether filter conditions are equivalent, whether one implies another, or whether they are mutually exclusive. An incorrect judgment can omit qualifying tuples or include nonqualifying ones. We introduce SemReuse, a system for safely reusing previous semantic-filter results. SemReuse stores each result as a materialized view and compares new filters with existing views to identify tuples that can provisionally be accepted or skipped without another LLM call. Because these decisions rely on a fallible language reasoner, SemReuse audits every rewritten result. Its pooled stratified audit uses exact finite-population bounds to provide jointly valid per-query recall and precision guarantees. If the requested guarantees cannot be established, SemReuse evaluates additional tuples and progressively approaches full evaluation. Thus, incorrect relation judgments affect efficiency rather than the validity of the reported guarantees. Experiments on large labeled corpora and real LLM executions show reductions of up to 15.4x. They also identify cross-filter inconsistency as the main source of repair cost.

### 5. The Case for Single-Version Concurrency Control in the Cloud

- arXiv ID: `2610.15793v1`
- Authors: Lam-Duy Nguyen, Gabriel Haas, Muhammad El-Hindi, Viktor Leis, Philip A. Bernstein
- Published: 2026-10-11
- Categories: `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15793v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15793v1)

Multi-version concurrency control (MVCC) has been the dominant concurrency protocol in modern database management systems (DBMSs). However, cloud-native HTAP architectures shift many of MVCC's traditional advantages from the primary transaction engine to the deployment architecture. Now, transactional and analytical processing are decoupled into loosely synchronized engines, while cloud deployment primitives provide the workload isolation required for this design. In this paper, we challenge the status quo, arguing that single-version concurrency control (SVCC) is better suited to cloud transaction processing than MVCC. Through a systematic analysis of MVCC's limitations, we show that SVCC avoids these shortcomings through a more lightweight and efficient design. Beyond arguing for a lighter transaction processing, we use this SVCC effort as a blueprint to advocate for a broader rethinking of cloud-native architectures and to outline a research roadmap to shape next-generation DBMSs.

### 6. TL;QR: Querying Temporal Discursive Patterns in Textual Corpora

- arXiv ID: `2610.15712v1`
- Authors: François Hublet, Vladimir Krsmanovic, Jesus Solano, Ahmed Bouhoula, Ana Oliveira da Costa
- Published: 2026-10-11
- Categories: `cs.CL`, `cs.CY`, `cs.DB`, `cs.LO`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15712v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15712v1)

Many questions about large textual corpora concern patterns that unfold over time, such as a political group whose stance on a topic drifts from support to criticism over several years of debates. We present TL;QR (Too Long; Query with logical temporal Reasoning), a declarative query language and open-source engine for finding such temporal discursive patterns. In TL;QR, a single query combines natural-language predicates, whose truth is judged by a Large Language Model (LLM), with regular expressions and the operators of a temporal logic. The LLM answers only local questions about individual text passages; a symbolic engine then aggregates these judgments over time and links every match to the recorded passages and predicate decisions behind it. With this division of labor, the temporal and relational reasoning stays exact and auditable even over long time horizons. In addition, when a query includes a regular-expression pre-filter, only a small fraction of the corpus ever reaches the LLM. On a suite of controlled synthetic benchmarks with temporally and semantically complex patterns, TL;QR is substantially more accurate than LLM-only baselines. In a case study on ten years of European Parliament debates (442,593 multilingual speeches), TL;QR identifies candidate shifts in the political groups' stances on the GDPR privacy regulation.

### 7. LYNX: Analytical Program Synthesis via Grounding and Closed-Loop Refinement

- arXiv ID: `2610.14777v1`
- Authors: Ruichen Xia, Xiao Han, Dingyu Yang, Luwei Yang, Sai Wu, Dongxiang Zhang, Binhui Yao, Gang Chen
- Published: 2026-10-10
- Categories: `cs.AI`, `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14777v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14777v1)

LLM-powered data analytics aims to translate natural language instructions into executable analytical programs over underlying data. However, existing approaches, whether treating LLMs as intent-to-code translators or as standalone analytical engines, often fail to reliably align domain semantics with complex schemas and heterogeneous metadata, resulting in brittle execution and semantically inconsistent analytical outputs in real-world workloads. We present LYNX, a multi-agent framework that formulates LLM-driven analytics as a schema- and knowledge-constrained analysis problem rather than a single-pass generation task. LYNX organizes analytical synthesis into a closed-loop workflow of planning, generation, verification, and refinement through three role-specialized agents that collaboratively enforce schema grounding, semantic consistency, and execution correctness. To address domain ambiguity and schema misalignment, LYNX integrates Semantic-Structural Distillation and Stage-Aware Allocation (SSD-SAA). A delta-guided iterative revision continuously diagnoses and repairs semantic and execution-level failures, substantially improving synthesis robustness. We evaluate LYNX on two NL-to-SQL benchmarks, BIRD-SQL Mini-Dev and the more challenging Spider 2.0-Lite. Experimental results demonstrate strong performance and efficiency, achieving 10.66% to 59.54% execution accuracy across lightweight and commercial models, while reducing token consumption by 70.53% compared with the prior most efficient method. Our framework displays high generalization over different backbones, from open-source, lightweight models to the latest commercial models. Additional case studies on quantitative finance analytics and complex multi-relational SQL workloads further demonstrate the framework's robustness, scalability, and practical applicability in real-world analytical scenarios.

### 8. TurboProm: Low Cost and High Performance Metrics Observability at Scale

- arXiv ID: `2610.14483v1`
- Authors: Milind Srivastava, Zeying Zhu, Peilin Xin, Yancheng Yuan, Zaoxing Liu, Vyas Sekar
- Published: 2026-10-10
- Categories: `cs.DB`, `cs.DC`, `cs.NI`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14483v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14483v1)

Many large enterprise and cloud deployments rely on metrics observability solutions (e.g., Prometheus) to monitor key performance indicators of system health (e.g., CPU, memory, network, app-level statistics). Monitoring and analyzing these metrics presents a significant challenge in terms of cost-latency tradeoffs at scale. Ideally, system operators would like immediately deployable and backwards compatible solutions to improve the cost-latency tradeoffs without significant impact on the fidelity of their observability applications. Unfortunately, existing solutions including state-of-art timeseries databases and custom approximate querying solutions fail on one or more aspects.
  In this paper, we present the design and implementation of TurboProm, a backwards-compatible drop-in accelerator for metrics observability deployments. TurboProm recasts metrics analytics tasks using a semantic-preserving summary view rather than classical query-centric view. Instead of estimating query results on raw timeseries metrics data, TurboProm uses semantic-preserving summaries that enable high-fidelity analytics with significant cost-latency improvements. We present a practical implementation of TurboProm that can be retrofitted to any existing observability deployment. We evaluate TurboProm on diverse real and synthetic workloads against state-of-art solutions and show that it can provide significant reduction in query latency and cost. TurboProm is open-source at https://github.com/ProjectASAP/ASAPQuery.

### 10. PreciseCoder: Faithful and Generalizable Debugging Beyond Unit-Test Rewards

- arXiv ID: `2610.18755v1`
- Authors: Yunkai Zhan, Miaosen Chai, Shangshang Wang, Jike Zhong, Yuqing Yang, Deqing Fu, Serina Chang, Wang Bill Zhu
- Published: 2026-10-13
- Categories: `cs.SE`, `cs.AI`, `cs.CL`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18755v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18755v1)

Reinforcement learning (RL) from unit tests is the common recipe for post-training code models, but a unit test only asks whether the final program passes. We analyze what this unit-test-only reward teaches a model during debugging and find that, while it raises pass rates, it also teaches the model to edit more and to hallucinate bugs in code that is already correct. We introduce PreciseCoder, a training algorithm that augments the unit-test reward with a signal for semantically verified, localized edits, which correlates with smaller edits and higher pass rates. On 4B, 9B, and 27B models, PreciseCoder reduces the average number of edit lines from 7-15to 3 at a pass rate on par with unit-test-only training. Trained only on single-line bugs, PreciseCoder can generalize to repository-level program repair, where it resolves more issues with smaller patches. Its precision also transfers from weaker to stronger models: given the reasoning trace of a 9B PreciseCoder model, frontier models such as GPT-5.6 Sol and Qwen3.8-Max gain 4.6-12.4 points in edit precision with no additional training. Linguistic analysis of the thinking traces shows that our reward encourages the model to reason about individual lines, name the buggy ones, and form consistent bug hypotheses.

### 11. X.js: A Live Benchmark for Node.js Taint-Style Vulnerabilities

- arXiv ID: `2610.18585v1`
- Authors: José Afonso, João Madeira Pereira, Ronghao Ni, Limin Jia, José Fragoso Santos, Pedro Adão
- Published: 2026-10-13
- Categories: `cs.CR`, `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18585v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18585v1)

JavaScript vulnerabilities in npm packages pose a significant risk to the Node.js ecosystem, and a growing body of work aims to detect, exploit, and mitigate them. Studying and evaluating this work requires up-to-date collections of real vulnerabilities, yet the most commonly used datasets stop recording vulnerabilities in 2022. We present X.js, a live benchmark of real-world Node.js taint-style vulnerabilities covering code and command injection, prototype pollution, and path traversal. Built from 1,738 advisories collected through mid-2026, primarily from the GitHub Advisory Database, X.js pairs each vulnerability with precise source and sink locations, a validated executable exploit and its execution trace, and, when available, a patched version of the package. X.js supports long-term reproducibility and growth: dependencies are mirrored in a self-hosted registry to guard against package removal from the ecosystem, and dedicated infrastructure generates and validates exploits for newly reported vulnerabilities. The current release contains 1,159 validated exploits for 965 packages. Using X.js, we characterize how the frequency, structure, and complexity of Node.js taint-style vulnerabilities have evolved over time. We find that these vulnerabilities are increasingly reported in large packages: the median vulnerable package grew from roughly 250 LoC in 2021--2022 to 6,100 LoC in 2026.

### 12. Soliduzz: Finding Miscompilations in Smart-Contract Compilers with Assertion-Driven Equivalence Oracles

- arXiv ID: `2610.18165v1`
- Authors: Alexander Stromberger, Christoph Hochrainer, Valentin Wüstholz, Maria Christakis
- Published: 2026-10-13
- Categories: `cs.SE`, `cs.PL`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18165v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18165v1)

Smart contracts compiled to the Ethereum Virtual Machine (EVM) can manage high-value assets and are difficult to patch after deployment. Compiler miscompilations are therefore especially dangerous: compilation succeeds, but the generated bytecode does not preserve the semantics of the source program. Existing smart-contract compiler fuzzers either focus on robustness failures or rely on differential oracles that compare compiler configurations.
  We present Soliduzz, an approach for finding miscompilations in EVM smart-contract compilers using assertion-driven equivalence oracles. Soliduzz uses product programs to encode semantic equivalence as executable assertions. The key shift is to compile these self-checking programs and let off-the-shelf fuzzers and symbolic execution engines explore the resulting bytecode for assertion violations, without requiring compiler-specific support from the analyzer.
  Soliduzz instantiates this idea with two complementary families of metamorphic transformations. The first starts from equivalent expressions and lifts them into smart-contract code that deliberately exercises EVM-specific features such as storage, data locations, control flow, contract interactions, and inline assembly. The second generalizes Equivalence Modulo Inputs (EMI) from fixed concrete inputs to symbolic input spaces. Our symbolic EMI records coverage in the original computation and uses it to guard mutations in the transformed computation. Together, these transformations produce product programs that assert that paired computations yield the same outputs.
  We evaluate Soliduzz on three EVM smart-contract compilers (solc, solx, and vyper) using several analysis tools. Overall, Soliduzz exposes 29 bugs. Of these, 24 are compiler bugs: 6 miscompilations, 17 internal compiler errors, and 1 hang. The remaining 5 are bugs in the analysis tools.

### 13. DeepGroundRepair: From API-Grounded Generation to Shape-Aware Repair for Deep Learning Programs

- arXiv ID: `2610.16897v1`
- Authors: Xiangyue Ma, Zhen Wang, Xuhui Lu, Xiaoting Du, Chenglong Li, Jun Li, Haotian Li, Zheng Zheng
- Published: 2026-10-12
- Categories: `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16897v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.16897v1)

Deep learning programs are fundamental to modern AI applications, and automatically generating them from natural language requirements can substantially reduce DL development effort. Large language models have demonstrated promising capabilities for this direction. However, generated DL programs often remain unreliable because they must satisfy domain specific API usage constraints and tensor shape dependencies, violations of which can cause API level syntactic issues and runtime failures. Existing retrieval augmented generation and feedback guided code repair methods do not adequately address these DL specific constraints.
  In this paper, we propose DeepGroundRepair, a three stage framework that establishes a generation localization repair workflow to improve the reliability of LLM-generated DL programs. Its key idea is to progressively narrow the generation and repair spaces using DL specific knowledge and runtime tensor information. Specifically, it grounds generation in task-relevant API knowledge, localizes the upstream causes of tensor shape mismatch failures, and uses runtime tensor shapes to guide parameter repair.
  We evaluate DeepGroundRepair on the 100 model-level DL programming tasks in DeepEval using six open-weight LLMs across TensorFlow, PyTorch, and PaddlePaddle. We compare it with four baselines: two generation-only methods and their counterparts incorporating traceback guided self debugging. The results show that, relative to these baselines, DeepGroundRepair reduces the combined number of API-level syntactic issues by an average of 5.4 per 10 program files and achieves an average relative improvement of 85.3% in code executability. Compared with the two self debugging baselines, it improves semantic consistency by an average of 18.1% and reduces token cost by an average of 28.4%.

### 14. When Agent Evaluation Becomes Unreliable: An Empirical Study of Oracle Failures and Configuration-Dependent Measurement in an LLM Agent Benchmark

- arXiv ID: `2610.16067v1`
- Authors: Junjie Chen
- Published: 2026-10-12
- Categories: `cs.SE`, `cs.AI`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16067v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.16067v1)

Agent evaluation harnesses are software systems that run test suites, generate inputs, and decide outcomes with test oracles. This paper audits such software for reliability defects that score tables cannot reveal.
  We introduce an oracle-integrity auditing approach with five state-integrity invariants and three known-answer controls. We evaluate it by injecting seven oracle defect classes across two task domains and a synthetic domain. All injected defect classes are detected, and the repaired oracle has no false positives. Applied to an agent benchmark, the audit reveals a reward-function defect that changes the environment state before post-run scoring. Across 4,320 episodes, replay-based correction changes database-pass labels from 4,194 to 2,883 and reduces measured success from 94.3% to 65.5%.
  We also study configuration-dependent measurement effects. A component ablation over 3,240 episodes shows that the agent's policy documents, tool descriptions, and tool error strings influence observed trajectory reliability, with tool descriptions showing the largest exploratory effect (-9.2 pp) and error strings the smallest (-5.3 pp). A second task domain reverses the sign of the language effect, so the result is bounded to the tested configuration.
  Finally, we report a portability boundary: the auditing approach cannot validate a second independently implemented benchmark because the required reference terminal state cannot be reconstructed from its recorded API-call log.
  The paper releases a replication package containing the auditing suite, fault-injection catalogue, frozen records, deviation logs, and offline analysis entry points without network access, API keys, or model calls.

### 15. Separating Statistical Inference from Experimental Runtime: An Architecture for Sequential Scientific Analysis

- arXiv ID: `2610.15824v1`
- Authors: Alexander Stepanov, Andrej Novikov
- Published: 2026-10-11
- Categories: `math.ST`, `cs.MS`, `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15824v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15824v1)

Sequential statistical procedures are increasingly embedded in scientific systems in which observations arrive incrementally from heterogeneous sources. In such systems the statistical update is only one part of the computation: observations must also be acquired, aligned across inputs, ordered, delivered, persisted, and replayed, and results must be passed to systems that display them or act on them. Sequential methods are nevertheless missing from the general software toolkit. They run inside proprietary experimentation platforms and commercial statistical packages, or exist as offline functions over complete data, and the batch fit interface that dominates machine-learning libraries has no place for a procedure that listens, keeps state, and decides when to stop. We propose an architecture that separates statistical inference from data topology, execution runtime, run lifecycle, persistence, and external action. Its central abstraction is the Sequential Procedure: a state-transition function that maps an inferential state, a valid statistical input, and a statistical random state to a new state and a scientific output. The procedure does not define how observations are acquired, paired, scheduled, stored, or acted upon. We specify the architecture semantically rather than through an object model, derive seven conformance invariants that any implementation can be tested against (among them synchronous/asynchronous, checkpoint, replay, and batch equivalence), and describe a dependency-free reference implementation in which all seven hold bitwise. Lorden's 2-SPRT for the Kiefer-Weiss problem serves as the reference procedure; the same contract accommodates sequential estimators, Bayesian filters, change-point detectors, and stateful predictive models.

### 16. Passing Tests, Missing Semantics: Evidence of Interpretive Lock-In in Plan-Based DataFrame Code Generation

- arXiv ID: `2610.15797v1`
- Authors:  Jingjing,  Liu, Dishu Yang, Hongbo Qin, Hansong Zhang
- Published: 2026-10-11
- Categories: `cs.AI`, `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15797v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15797v1)

Planning and revision are intended to bring generated code closer to the task specification. Yet a mistaken interpretation can persist across the plan, implementation, and tests - a pattern we call interpretive lock-in. We investigate this risk on 30 DataFrame tasks derived from TPC Benchmark DS, comparing Frozen One-Pass, Test-Guided Revision, and PlanAudit-Guided Revision. Test-pass counts rose from 22/30 to 29/30 and 30/30, yet no generated solution matched its stored reference output. Evaluator controls returned the expected outcome in 57/57 cases. Same-engine SQL re-execution reproduced 28/30 references; all 77 test-passing runs on these tasks still failed reference agreement. PlanAudit-Guided Revision improved code scores on 3/30 tasks and test coverage on 11/30, but the median paired change was zero for both measures. Failure analysis revealed tests that omitted requirements, adopted candidate-defined schemas, used nondiscriminating fixtures, or repeated the implementation's incorrect logic. Fresh generation on ten targeted tasks produced 0/30 reference matches and 25/25 false passes among test-passing runs. These findings are diagnostic rather than causal: the pipelines differ in feedback, grounding, call budgets, and editing permissions, and the study uses one model and one primary generation per eligible task. Because pre-adjudication human labels were not retained, inter-rater agreement cannot be reconstructed and the mechanism analyses remain exploratory. Overall, increasingly successful self-generated tests did not establish semantic correctness, even after excluding unreproduced references.

### 17. LLM-based Code Auditing by Inferring and Evaluating Developer Assumptions

- arXiv ID: `2610.15729v1`
- Authors: Junming Liu, Marcel Böhme
- Published: 2026-10-11
- Categories: `cs.CR`, `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15729v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15729v1)

When we see a null pointer check, we know that the developer believes that the pointer may not be initialized. If the same pointer is later used without a null pointer check, we may consider this a violation of the developer's belief and ultimately a bug. We develop such a belief-guided program analysis for vulnerability detection (VD). For example, when an authentication function returns the same error for both, a wrong user name and a wrong password, an auditor may infer that the developer believes that both cases are indistinguishable, mitigating a user enumeration attack. This belief is reasonable, as it follows standard security practice. Yet, a function call inducing a difference in response time for both cases, contradicts this belief. This contradiction uncovers a timing side-channel that syntactic analysis tools would miss. Existing static analysis tools struggle with this type of flaw because they require them to be expressible by general, syntactic patterns. Our belief-guided approach detects such domain-specific, high-level semantic security flaws that remain elusive.
  Given a function, our approach, Creed, infers the developer's security-related beliefs, evaluates how reasonable each one is, and derives the conditions under which it is violated. In contrast to existing ML-based VD techniques, Creed explains the root cause, the symptoms, and the conditions under which each issue is exploitable. To address the context-dependency problem in the evaluation, we introduce two measures for explanation-based VD: recall quantifies how effectively an approach correctly explains a known vulnerability, and overhead quantifies the effort of inspecting its reports. On a recent real-world vulnerability benchmark, Creed detects both semantic and syntactic vulnerabilities more effectively than existing explanation-based VD tools, achieving the best effectiveness-effort trade-off.

### 18. When Messages Go Wrong: Understanding Communication Faults in LLM-Based Multi-Agent Systems

- arXiv ID: `2610.15459v1`
- Authors: Jinhao Zhou, Xiaoyu Zhang, Jiongchi Yu, Fahao Chen, Jun Wu
- Published: 2026-10-11
- Categories: `cs.SE`, `cs.AI`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15459v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15459v1)

LLM-based multi-agent systems (LLM-MAS) use specialized agents that exchange messages to accomplish complex tasks. Their reliance on networked protocols, remote tools, and model endpoints exposes communication to message loss, delay, and context expiration. Because agents often exchange open-ended natural language, anomalies can alter task-relevant information while leaving messages processable, allowing semantic errors to propagate through subsequent interactions. Systematic studies of faults in inter-agent communication remain limited. This paper presents a large-scale empirical study of how communication faults manifest, propagate, and affect task outcomes in LLM-MAS. We construct a cross-layer taxonomy linking lower-stack disturbances to application-visible faults and develop a fault-injection and tracing pipeline. Our evaluation covers more than 29,000 executions across 100 tasks, five domains, three workflow topologies, and three model configurations. We find that 20.7% of failures with complete detection records occur without an immediate failure signal, reaching 76.2% for partial-content faults. Flat maintains 53.0% task success under communication faults, compared with 3.3% and 3.2% for Sequential and Hierarchical, respectively. This advantage primarily arises when an alternative information path preserves the evidence required by downstream agents. We also evaluate execution-side and evidence protection mechanisms and find that they recover different failure cases. Evidence recovery depends on whether trustworthy information remains accessible through an unaffected path. These observations provide empirical insights into communication-fault propagation, workflow resilience, and protection design for LLM-MAS.
