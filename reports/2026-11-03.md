### 1. SQLoom: Set-Contrastive Learning of Syntactic, Semantic, and Logical Dependencies for Text-to-SQL

- arXiv ID: `2611.03298v1`
- Authors: Hao Fu, Degang Sun, Wan Wei, Jinxia Wei, Chun Long, Peng Gao
- Published: 2026-11-02
- Categories: `cs.DB`, `cs.AI`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03298v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03298v1)

Text-to-SQL translates natural language questions into SQL queries, enabling database access without writing SQL. Fine-tuning open-weight large language models (LLMs) is cost-effective, but existing methods treat SQL as ordinary text and optimize token-level likelihood without explicitly modeling its syntactic, semantic, and logical requirements. Thus, a single incorrect alias or comparison operator can invalidate a query while contributing little to training loss. We propose SQLoom, a constraint-aware fine-tuning framework with supervision at three levels: syntactic validity under SQL grammar and database schema, semantic alignment between query decisions and the question, and logical consistency across dependent clause decisions. SQLoom extends supervised fine-tuning with a unified set-contrastive objective favoring sets of correct outputs over minimally different incorrect ones at each level. LLM agents propose this supervision, which a SQL parser and execution on counterexample databases verify. We also release Spider-Refined, a training dataset correcting 378 flawed Spider annotations and enriching database schemas. Experiments on Spider, BIRD, and three perturbed Spider variants show that SQLoom improves execution accuracy for Qwen3-1.7B from 62.9% zero-shot to 88.7% on Spider development, reaching 89.2% on Spider test and 73.4% on BIRD development. It outperforms fine-tuning, reinforcement learning, and agent-based baselines with up to 70 billion parameters. On question perturbations, SQLoom loses at most 0.6 accuracy points versus up to 12.1 for agent-based methods. It generates SQL in a single pass at 0.42s per query, 23$\times$ faster than a GPT-4-based agent method. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of set-contrastive supervision over token-level training.

### 2. Why Tabular Foundation Models Excel on Relational Data They Have Never Seen

- arXiv ID: `2611.02936v1`
- Authors: Linjie Xu, Yiqun Hu, Jixiang He, David Wipf
- Published: 2026-11-02
- Categories: `cs.AI`, `cs.DB`, `cs.LG`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02936v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02936v1)

Given a relational database (RDB) storing heterogeneous tabular information, how can we predict missing (or future) values in some target column of interest? As candidate targets are numerous in enterprise settings, it is preferable to avoid learning a new model from scratch each time there is a new prediction task. Frozen foundation models (FMs) based on in-context learning (ICL) and RDB-specific pre-training provide a viable solution, but the ideal design still remains largely unresolved. Perhaps unexpectedly, preliminary evidence has recently shown that existing single-table FMs (aka TFMs), when combined with suitable parameter-free relational encoders, can be quite competitive in practice despite zero exposure to RDB pre-training or relational data of any kind. In this work we first empirically confirm this phenomenon with a more diverse array of experiments. We then turn to our main contribution, namely, surfacing multiple complementary explanations for why TFMs behave so effectively. These explanations ultimately hinge on innate synergies whereby parameter-free encoders map relational effects to fixed-length embeddings that loosely mimic distributions for which TFMs were originally designed; in so doing, desirable single-table properties transfer to relational settings (e.g., convergent posterior predictive distributions). Collectively, our analysis forges new pathways for improving RDB FMs, particularly when we prioritize interpretability and inevitable integration within agentic systems.

### 3. Stop Handcrafting Your Engine! GenDB-V: Towards AI-Generated and Formally Verified Database Systems

- arXiv ID: `2611.02779v1`
- Authors: Jiale Lao, Immanuel Trummer
- Published: 2026-11-02
- Categories: `cs.DB`, `cs.AI`, `cs.CL`, `cs.LG`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02779v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02779v1)

For over 50 years, the database community has developed a wide range of successful engines to answer two fundamental questions: how can database systems run faster, and how can we ensure their correctness? Experience with these engines shows that performance depends on hardware and workloads. As both change over time, we repeatedly redesign and rebuild database systems, a time-consuming and expensive process. Correctness remains another long-standing challenge: recent studies continue to uncover wrong-answer bugs even in mature database engines, despite years of development and testing. To address these, we present GenDB-V, a system that enables AI agents to synthesize database engines specialized to hardware and workloads for performance, and formally verified for correctness. GenDB-V supports specialized implementations of SQL templates and general-purpose engines that accept any query within a supported SQL subset. First, we develop SQLSpec to formalize SQL semantics and deterministically construct fixed, implementation-agnostic synthesis targets. Second, we enable joint synthesis of fast code and formal proofs through storage--execution co-optimization, performance-first exploration, and three-layer modular proofs that bridge low-level execution to SQLSpec semantics. Third, we verify the synthesized code and proofs against the fixed synthesis targets in an isolated pipeline, proving SQL correctness and program safety. We evaluate GenDB-V on TPC-H and SEC-EDGAR. For verified template implementations, GenDB-V achieves geometric-mean speedups of 22.0-61.3x over DuckDB and Umbra. For a verified general-purpose engine, GenDB-V also achieves speedups of 1.27-3.4x on TPC-H. GenDB-V also achieves better performance than a testing-based LLM synthesis method, while our audit and tests expose incorrect answers in that method's implementations for 16 of 28 templates.

### 4. High-Throughput Private Information Retrieval with on-CPU Accelerators

- arXiv ID: `2611.02376v1`
- Authors: Gyuyeong Kim
- Published: 2026-11-02
- Categories: `cs.OS`, `cs.CR`, `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02376v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02376v1)

Private information retrieval (PIR) hides which record a client retrieves by scanning the entire database, and batching is an effective technique to improve throughput by sharing this scan across queries. However, existing CPU-based solutions gain little from batching because each query still requires its own multiplications, and vector instructions perform only a few at a time. GPUs and other discrete accelerators can accelerate these computations, but require extra hardware and can constrain database size due to their limited memory capacity. In this paper, we present AltoPIR, the first PIR system that leverages on-CPU matrix accelerators such as Intel AMX. AltoPIR keeps the accelerator fed from the CPU caches, reading each database byte from memory once for all queries in a batch and never writing intermediate products to memory. This enables high-performance PIR through efficient batching without requiring discrete accelerators. Our evaluation shows that AltoPIR achieves up to 50.1x higher throughput than existing CPU-based solutions and serves databases larger than a GPU's memory.

### 5. Mixed-Type Relational Database Synthesis with Graph-Prompted Language Model

- arXiv ID: `2611.02373v1`
- Authors: Yuxiang Sun, Mengshi Chen, Jianwei Wang, Wenjie Zhang, Kai Wang
- Published: 2026-11-02
- Categories: `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02373v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02373v1)

Data access is often limited by privacy or proprietary restrictions, motivating relational database synthesis, which synthesizes realistic contents across related tables. Relational databases couple multi-table structure with mixed-type attributes and intricate semantic dependencies among values, making realistic synthesis challenging. In this paper, we propose REMIX, a unified framework for realistic relational database synthesis with mixed-type attributes. REMIX integrates a small language model (SLM) and a large language model (LLM) through two key phases: Graph-to-Prompt Tuning and Collaborative SLM-LLM Decoding. To capture attribute dependencies within and across tables, Graph-to-Prompt Tuning represents the database as an attributed heterogeneous graph. We project mixed-type record embeddings and aggregated relational context into soft prompts that serve as continuous vectors for an SLM. We jointly optimize the graph-to-prompt mapping and SLM through autoregressive attribute reconstruction. Furthermore, to reduce semantic inconsistencies, Collaborative SLM-LLM Decoding combines SLM-based record generation with repair programs induced by an LLM. The LLM discovers semantic dependencies, iteratively refines them through independent validation with a statistical bound on the overall probability of false acceptance, and translates validated dependencies into tested programs that repair generated values. Extensive experiments on seven real-world relational databases demonstrate the effectiveness of REMIX in generating high-fidelity data with improved semantic consistency and downstream utility.

### 6. TSSDA: An Autonomous Agent for Constructing a Time-Series Text-to-SQL Benchmark

- arXiv ID: `2611.02132v1`
- Authors: Zhihan Zheng, Shaoxu Song, Haitao Yuan, Minxiao Chen
- Published: 2026-11-01
- Categories: `cs.LG`, `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02132v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02132v1)

As agents increasingly undertake data querying, Text-to-SQL serves as a foundational capability for database access. However, for text-to-SQL in time-series databases, high-quality benchmarks and large-scale training corpora remain scarce, limiting the systematic evaluation and domain adaptation of existing models and agents. Existing automated SQL generation approaches lack sufficient autonomy, leaving key challenges unresolved. First, numerous combinations of temporal syntax and functions complicate comprehensive coverage of valid queries. Second, as the corpus grows, generating valuable and diverse new data becomes increasingly challenging. Third, schema and data heterogeneity hinder reuse of temporal query structures, limiting generation efficiency. To address these challenges, we propose TSSDA, a Time-Series SQL (TSSQL) Generation Data Agent framework, and construct TERN, a comprehensive Time-sERies text-to-SQL beNchmark. First, to achieve broad coverage of valid queries, we systematically model TSSQL syntax and semantics to construct a data generation space. Next, to explore the space and improve diversity, we develop a Data Agent that autonomously orchestrates data generation. The agent comprises a planning and decision-making module and a query generation module, with online adaptation for continuous self-improvement. Finally, we introduce a temporal-aware memory that enables adaptive reuse of experience across heterogeneous data instances, improving generation efficiency. Comprehensive evaluations on TERN reveal the limitations of existing methods in performing Text-to-TSSQL tasks. Furthermore, extensive experiments demonstrate that TSSDA substantially improves coverage, diversity, and complexity.

### 7. Graphs Have Structure: Declarative Manifolds, 3D Z-Order Monoid Roll-ups, and Zero-Simulation Visual Analytics in a Graph DBMS

- arXiv ID: `2611.01879v1`
- Authors: Madhulatha Mandarapu, Sandeep Kunkunuru
- Published: 2026-11-01
- Categories: `cs.DB`, `cs.CG`, `cs.HC`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01879v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.01879v1)

Every real-world graph is embedded in an ambient structural manifold: proteins fold in Euclidean space ($R^3$), telecom networks sit on a planetary surface ($S^2 \times R$), and ontologies and calendars form discrete subsumption posets ($(V, \sqsubseteq)$). Yet property graph databases treat node coordinates as opaque payload scalars, leave spatial proximity edges unindexed, and force visualizers into $O(V^2)$ client-side force-directed simulations that destroy true geometry and truncate hierarchy roll-ups. We elevate ambient manifold structure to a first-class primitive in Samyama Graph. First, we introduce a declarative and auto-detected structural convention contract (samyama.structure/1) persisted in .sgsnap headers that binds node labels to Euclidean 3D, Geo-Spherical, Causal Spacetime, or Poset Hierarchy manifolds. Second, we show that both discrete posets (via Order-Embedded Hierarchy $[tin, tout]$ DFS intervals) and continuous 3D manifolds (via a 63-bit 3D Morton Z-order Structural-Geometric Index, SGI) reduce regional aggregation to 1D rank-interval monoid folds answered directly from index-resident Fenwick trees (SUM, COUNT) and Segment Trees (MIN, MAX). At $100,000$ 3D nodes, SGI builds in $28.14$ ms and answers Morton-interval SUM and MAX roll-ups in $0.048\,μ$s and $0.104\,μ$s---$8,345.1\times$ and $3,824.4\times$ faster than property scans. Third, we introduce 3D voxel-hashed virtual contact edges weighted by Topological--Geometric Stress $σ(u,v) = d_{graph}(u,v) / d_{manifold}(u,v)$ to isolate long-range tertiary folds, plus manifold-guided $A^*$ shortest-path search that cuts explored states by up to $3.80\times$ over Dijkstra. Fourth, we replace client layout simulation with server-computed 3D Cone-Tree/Ribbon coordinates and a non-WebGL 2.5D Isometric projection with painter's-order depth sorting and sub-millisecond Level-of-Detail (LoD) macro-cuts.

### 8. Federated Vector Retrieval across Heterogeneous VectorDBs

- arXiv ID: `2611.01514v1`
- Authors: Mintaek Lim, Sieun Hyeon, Geon Chio, Jaeyoung Do
- Published: 2026-11-01
- Categories: `cs.DB`, `cs.IR`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01514v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.01514v1)

Vector databases are now core infrastructure for AI retrieval systems, yet many real deployments must serve queries over multiple independently constructed vectorDBs built with different embedding models. This creates a systems problem of federated retrieval over heterogeneous vectorDBs: vectors and similarity scores are not directly comparable across embedding spaces, while fully resolving this incompatibility through corpus-wide re-embedding and re-indexing is often operationally prohibitive. Existing methods therefore face a difficult trade-off between retrieval quality, construction cost, and query throughput. We present HVMap, a lightweight compatibility layer for federated retrieval across heterogeneous vectorDBs. Instead of rebuilding databases into a unified space, HVMap learns bidirectional mappings between embedding spaces using a fuzzy contrastive loss, enabling calibrated cross-database scoring without corpus migration or index reconstruction. HVMap is system-agnostic and further scales to multi-vectorDB settings while preserving ranking fidelity through a hub-based architecture. We evaluate HVMap in open-domain, cross-domain, and scale-out settings, and also construct a new benchmark for heterogeneous cross-domain federation. Across these settings, HVMap consistently achieves a better quality--cost--throughput trade-off than baselines, achieving up to 3.59X higher nDCG@10, up to 2.01X higher query throughput, and construction time as low as 0.3% of baseline methods. These results show that HVMap makes heterogeneous vectorDB federation practical at scale.

### 10. Eliminating Servers in State Machine Replication

- arXiv ID: `2611.01363v1`
- Authors: Dohyun Park, Wenjie Hu, Xiangyao Yu, Mahesh Balakrishnan, Yongjoo Park
- Published: 2026-11-01
- Categories: `cs.DB`, `cs.DC`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01363v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.01363v1)

State Machine Replication (SMR) underpins many stateful systems, including distributed databases, coordination services, and metadata stores. Fundamentally, SMR requires state to be materialized from a durable total order of commands. Existing SMR systems rely on servers to materialize, locally store, and serve snapshots of state; while the durable total order either resides on the same servers, or on an external, disaggregated shared log. Unfortunately, servers act as brittle, expensive choke-points in such systems, causing service unavailability when they fail; incurring provisioning latency on cold starts; and red-lining when exposed to bad clients. In this paper, we propose Serverless SMR (S^2MR), where both the materialized state and the durable total order are stored on disaggregated storage; clients concurrently maintain a single copy of materialized state on external storage. Our key technical insight is that the shared log abstraction is useful not just for consistently ordering commands in SMR, but also for enabling distributed materialization, thus becoming the only interface for ac hieving SMR. We demonstrate S^2MR by developing AirDB, a transactional key-value store. In our evaluation, AirD B over S^2MR achieves higher availability than existing SMR-based systems like etcd over Raft: it is oblivious to server failures and bad client activity; and spins up 10x-45x faster from a cold start.

### 11. Self-Refining Prompt Induction for Streaming Text-to-SQL

- arXiv ID: `2611.00960v1`
- Authors: Gaetano Rossiello, Alfio Gliozzo, Nandana Mihindukulasooriya, Erick Lee, Nabila Siregar, Lisa Amini
- Published: 2026-10-31
- Categories: `cs.CL`, `cs.AI`, `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00960v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.00960v1)

Text-to-SQL assistants for streaming engines must handle dialect-specific types, windowing rules, and temporal semantics, including constructs poorly handled by generic prompts. We present Self-Refining Prompt Induction (SRPI), an offline method that converts generation failures into reusable dialect instructions while keeping model weights fixed. A Refiner revises the Generator's prompt using reference-conditioned feedback from three LLM judges; a sampled regression guard checks earlier successes, and a separate Compressor reduces prompt length. Inference uses the frozen prompt without retrieval or query repair. We evaluate cross-domain and cross-model transfer on Confluent Cloud Flink SQL using a controlled synthetic workload seeded by 30 expert-written examples, with 335 induction examples and 257 questions from held-out domains. The same induced prompt improves mean judge scores across seven Generators by 7.1-19.2 percentage points, including an increase from 85.3% to 97.5% for the induction model. A separate compression study reduces prompt length by 52.7% with a 0.19-percentage-point score decrease. An assistant using the induced prompt is in early access, with initial user feedback to inform subsequent refinement.

### 12. NovaFilter - A Rule-Based ETL Staging Framework for Fault-Tolerant Relational Databases

- arXiv ID: `2611.00959v1`
- Authors: Meike Klettke
- Published: 2026-10-31
- Categories: `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00959v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.00959v1)

NovaFilter is a completely new approach to deriving structured relational data from heterogeneous input data in different data formats. It combines consistency checks, rule-based data transformation, and a human-in-the-loop example discussion. Using these three components, NovaFilter stores all transformation knowledge in an explicit rule base, which is continuously expanded through the example discussion with the user. The resulting data transformation process is deterministic, transparent, and reusable. As a result, the method can replace conventional ETL scripts and offers a completely new strategy for transforming data between different data models and schemas. Its main advantage is that users without programming skills (e.g. domain experts) can answer the example discussion and thus it is a building block for democratization of ETL script developement.

### 15. Modern Software Engineering with Verification Agents: A Spec-First Architecture for Autonomous Synthesis, Verification IRs, and Organizational Scale

- arXiv ID: `2611.02285v1`
- Authors: Jason Hickey
- Published: 2026-11-01
- Categories: `cs.SE`, `cs.LO`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02285v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02285v1)

When autonomous coding agents synthesize implementation code, formal specifications, and mechanical proofs simultaneously, the software engineering hierarchy inverts: the formal specification becomes the primary human-reviewed artifact, while source code and proof scripts become disposable build outputs. This paper presents an end-to-end architecture for agent-authored, formally verified software engineering. First, we establish a logic-agnostic foundation combining a spec-first stack inversion, a zero-code-review workflow gated by automated continuous integration (CI) checks, and a minimal Verification Intermediate Representation (VIR) lowered via a thin, verified translator. Second, we pair an extensional Computational Type Theory (CTT) specification layer with Higher-Order Abstract Syntax (HOAS) and linear resource typing in the VIR--eliminating rewrite friction for synthesis agents while defining an experiment, its protocol frozen before the first reader, to measure whether removing boundary coercions improves human specification readability. Third, we scale collaboration across multi-team organizations using Personal Spec-Copilots and a Shared Synthesis Fleet that detects cross-team Semantic Merge Conflicts and fuses modular contracts into zero-copy binaries. We illustrate this architecture on a zero-copy authenticated message router and distinguish our existing empirical baselines (salt, SaltBench, x86lean, and MetaPRL) from newly proposed components.

### 16. Semantic Interoperability in Federated Digital Twin Systems: A Systematic Review Through the Levels of Conceptual Interoperability Model

- arXiv ID: `2611.02125v1`
- Authors: Manonmani Sekar, Nasim Nezamoddini
- Published: 2026-11-01
- Categories: `cs.SE`, `cs.AI`, `eess.SY`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02125v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.02125v1)

When two companies connect their digital twins, software models of their plants kept up to date with live data, each twin must translate the other's data into its own terms, and that translation rule, called a mapping, can stop being correct when one company changes the meaning of its data without telling the other. This PRISMA 2020 systematic review screened 5,833 records and read 119 full texts to determine whether any published system detects such a broken mapping and repairs it while the twins operate. In all 32 included studies the mapping is fixed before operation, and the two ingredients of run-time repair appear only separately: cross-company matching without any check that the match still holds, and detection and repair of changed meaning within a single company. The review turns this gap into six requirements, each illustrated on one running example, that a system claiming run-time semantic interoperability between independently owned twins should meet.

### 17. From Stories to Bugs: Story-Based Semantic Fuzzing of Interactive Systems

- arXiv ID: `2611.01173v1`
- Authors: Yeshayahu Weiss, Achiya Elyasaf, Gera Weiss
- Published: 2026-10-31
- Categories: `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01173v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.01173v1)

Complex interactive systems hide business-logic failures along two complementary axes: deep state-dependent behaviors that require long valid prefixes and concurrency-sensitive failures that depend on the ordering of otherwise legal operations. We present story-based semantic fuzzing, a methodology that treats semantically constrained compositions of behavioral stories as primary search objects. Implemented through Behavioral Programming, semantic binding and state constraints preserve legal prefixes, while event eligibility, schedule selection, and replay expose failure-relevant interleavings. We evaluate the methodology on Magento and four stateful REST APIs. On Magento, overlap-aware ranking increased reproduction of a sporadic Shipping Fee race from 3.6% to 41.2%; a controlled ordering intervention reduced a second failure signature from 100/100 initial executions to 0/200 constrained executions. For the REST study, a versioned end-to-end pipeline took each frozen public OpenAPI contract as its sole benchmark-specific model-generation input and automatically generated the Basic and Complex stories, dependency structure, and interface bindings. Under a shared black-box task and sequence-local evaluation contract, Basic and Complex confirmed 0/11 and 11/11 semantic fault classes, while RESTler and EvoMaster confirmed 2/11 and 0/11. A paired binding ablation produced 250/250 valid dependent operations with binding and 0/250 without it, while supplementary prefix controls localized the difference to preservation of legal relational state. Together, the studies show that searching admissible semantic compositions provides a unified and effective representation for reaching deep states and exposing concurrency-sensitive business-logic failures.
