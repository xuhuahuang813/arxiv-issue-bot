### 1. FlinkEval: A Synthetic Corpus for Evaluating Natural Language Interfaces to Streaming SQL

- arXiv ID: `2611.17093v1`
- Authors: Alfio Massimiliano Gliozzo, Nandana Mihindukulasooriya, Gaetano Rossiello, Erick Lee, Lisa Amini
- Published: 2026-11-12
- Categories: `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17093v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17093v1)

Existing natural-language-to-SQL benchmarks primarily evaluate queries over bounded relational data, leaving streaming semantics underexplored. We introduce FlinkEval, an execution-grounded synthetic benchmark comprising 3,903 question--SQL pairs across 45 industry domains and 45 semantic labels. We create diverse schemas and domain-plausible, temporally consistent data through iterative generation and validation. SQL patterns collected from the human-written NL2SQL pairs and vendor documentation drive quota-balanced generation, and execution with a repair loop on Confluent Cloud Apache Flink produces reference queries covering windowing, temporal joins, pattern matching, nested types, and data manipulation. The corpus includes paired schema-explicit and business-language requests, with annotations for analysis by query pattern, complexity, and execution mode. A seven-level Question Specification Ladder examines semantics-preserving reformulation and underspecified requests augmented with oracle-derived clues. Baseline evaluations combine execution outcomes with adjudicated LLM judgments, revealing challenges in pattern composition and request specification.

### 2. [Experiment, Analysis, and Benchmark] How well do LLMs comprehend and design relational database schemata?

- arXiv ID: `2611.17044v1`
- Authors: Sriom Chakrabarti, Sean Bin Yang, Arijit Khan, Sebastian Link
- Published: 2026-11-12
- Categories: `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17044v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17044v1)

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly used for database tasks, yet most work, such as Text2SQL, assumes that the relational schema already exists. We ask whether LLMs can design a schema from natural language requirements (Text2Schema), verbalize an existing schema (Schema2Text), and preserve its structure through a Schema-to-Text-to-Schema round trip. We present SchemaBridge, a benchmark of 902 schemata from three collections, stratified by size and constraint complexity, that scores fidelity separately for tables, attributes, domain (data types and nullability), entity, and referential integrity. Evaluating six open-weight and proprietary LLMs, we find that: (i) human-written requirements under-specify schemata, with no model exceeding 0.27 referential integrity F1; (ii) LLM-generated descriptions carry far more structure when explicitly asked to be complete, raising GPT-5.4-mini's referential F1 on SchemaBench from 0.228 to 0.909, even as BLEU, ROUGE-L, and BERTScore decrease; (iii) controlled experiments show that both constructor and describer choices influence round-trip fidelity under fixed settings; and (iv) masked schema experiments show that structural cues support recovery of keys and relationships, while joint data-type and nullability information is poorly retained through verbalization.
  Code and data are available at https://github.com/iamsriom/SchemaBridge

### 3. Authenticated Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search in Outsourced Vector Databases

- arXiv ID: `2611.16516v1`
- Authors: Yuanhang Zhou, Xinyi Zhang, Cheng Xu, Jianliang Xu
- Published: 2026-11-12
- Categories: `cs.DB`, `cs.CR`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16516v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16516v1)

Approximate Nearest Neighbor (ANN) search has become a fundamental query primitive in modern vector databases for managing large-scale embedding data. To support growing vector collections, organizations increasingly outsource database management and query processing to third-party clouds. However, untrusted service providers may return suboptimal or fabricated results, posing a serious threat to ANN search integrity. Existing authenticated ANN approaches face substantial scalability challenges. They typically require transmitting high-dimensional vectors for distance verification and rely on topology-agnostic authentication structures, resulting in excessive communication and verification overhead. In this paper, we propose VIDA, a novel and efficient scheme for authenticated graph-based ANN search. VIDA replaces raw vector transmission with compact vector commitments and employs an aggregated Inner Product Argument to efficiently verify multiple distance computations. Furthermore, we introduce MeST, a topology-aware authenticated data structure that aligns authentication paths with graph traversal, significantly reducing structural proof overhead. Additionally, we design a quantization-aware authentication strategy that exploits distance bounds for pruning, reducing the number of exact distances requiring cryptographic authentication. Extensive experiments demonstrate that the proposed schemes substantially improve the efficiency of authenticated ANN search, achieving up to a $6.6\times$ speedup in end-to-end latency and reducing data transfer time by up to 99.2% compared with state-of-the-art methods.

### 4. REWEAVE: Online Learning for Workload-Adaptive HNSW Indexes

- arXiv ID: `2611.16060v1`
- Authors: Minghao Li, Nick Koudas, Raghav Mittal
- Published: 2026-11-11
- Categories: `cs.DB`, `cs.IR`, `cs.LG`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16060v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16060v1)

Vector databases rely on Hierarchical Navigable Small World (HNSW) indexes for similarity search, but a graph built from data alone can be poorly matched to a skewed or changing query workload. We present REWEAVE (Reward-Guided Rewiring of Vector Graphs), an online index-maintenance method that learns edge utility from query feedback and periodically rewires the bottom layer within the original per-node degree cap. The method supports sparse exact-neighbor feedback combined with self-labels, and standard HNSW serving without learning overhead once adaptation is disabled.
  Under explicit assumptions we prove value-estimation and graph-stabilization guarantees. Under stationary skew, REWEAVE improves held-out recall by up to 23.3 percentage points over HNSW. In comparisons with query-aware baselines on up to 100M vectors, it achieves up to 3.3x NGFix's query throughput at recall@100 of 0.80. On Amazon-ESCI with query drift, it increases stream recall@100 from 0.745 to 0.979 with 34% fewer search distance computations than frozen HNSW. These results illustrate the potential to improve retrieval quality and reduce search effort within an existing graph-degree budget, while incurring additional work during adaptation.

### 5. Towards Unified File Management for Data Lakehouse Systems

- arXiv ID: `2611.15975v1`
- Authors: Keonwoo Oh, Akshay Mehra, Joshua Kimball, Amol Deshpande
- Published: 2026-11-11
- Categories: `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15975v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15975v1)

Data lakehouse systems have garnered widespread adoption, yet their metadata management systems face a fundamental architectural dilemma. File-based OTFs (Open Table Formats) scale well, but suffer from metadata bloat, high commit latencies, the absence of multi-table transactions, and unsafe garbage collection. On the other hand, catalogs relying entirely on a transactional database incur severe scalability bottlenecks due to analytic file-level metadata. To reconcile this tension, we present Raven, an operational catalog that stores transactional metadata within a database while offloading analytical metadata to underlying OTF structures. Based on this hybrid architecture, Raven introduces an extended Merge-on-Read (MoR) mechanism to reduce commit latency and mitigate metadata bloat, alongside multi-table transactions under snapshot isolation. Furthermore, Raven unifies core data lakehouse semantics including versioning and reference tracking under an extensible ER (Entity-Relationship) metadata model, enabling safe global garbage collection across diverse types of assets. Empirical evaluations demonstrate that Raven substantially reduces commit latency and metadata bloat when integrated with Apache Iceberg and Delta Lake, maintains a catalog storage footprint orders of magnitude smaller than database-only systems like DuckLake, and achieves scalable garbage collection with minimal disruption.

### 6. HeriKGQA: Grounded Knowledge-Graph Question Answering over Cultural Heritage Sensor Data

- arXiv ID: `2611.15904v1`
- Authors: Lingxiao Kong, Vasileios Arampatzakis, Vasileios Sevetlidis, George Pavlidis, Zeyd Boukhers
- Published: 2026-11-11
- Categories: `cs.CL`, `cs.AI`, `cs.DB`, `cs.IR`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15904v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15904v1)

Cultural heritage sites are increasingly monitored by sensor networks, but querying their data requires database expertise. Large Language Models (LLMs) offer a natural-language interface, yet over raw sensor records they lose track of which sensor, site, and time each value belongs to, and silently miscompute. We present HeriKGQA, a knowledge-graph question-answering method that confines the LLM to interpreting questions and phrasing answers, leaving computation to SPARQL and deterministic code. Questions are parsed into intents constrained by a heritage ontology aligning W3C SOSA/SSN with CIDOC-CRM, compiled into SPARQL, and answered from query results and temporal, spatial, and anomaly facts derived by a grounding layer, with error correction guarding each LLM step. On 500 questions over real data from five heritage sites, HeriKGQA reaches 99.0% accuracy, 18.2 points above the strongest of three baselines using the same LLM and receiving the same anomaly rules and answer conventions, with comparable answer quality while running almost four times faster. Without grounding, accuracy drops by 12.2 points and on anomaly questions from 99% to 66%. We report lessons from development and live deployment.

### 7. VecDBLens: An Evaluatology-Based Framework for Vector Database Diagnosis

- arXiv ID: `2611.15105v1`
- Authors: Yutong Zhou, Guoxin Kang, Wenxin Zhou, Haoyu He, Yuedong Zhu, Lei Wang, Wanling Gao, Fanda Fan, Jianfeng Zhan
- Published: 2026-11-11
- Categories: `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15105v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15105v1)

Vector databases are increasingly deployed as retrieval pipelines in which representation, indexing, and query-time execution jointly determine accuracy, throughput, and cost. Existing vector database evaluations primarily focus on indexing while treating embeddings as fixed inputs and ignoring the role of query-time execution, making it difficult to decide whether a bottleneck should be addressed by changing the embedding model, the index, or the query-processing functions. To address the above limitation, we introduce VecDBLens, an Evaluatology-based framework that treats the function under diagnosis as an evaluated object and all remaining dataset, workload, parameter, and runtime factors as matched evaluation conditions. VecDBLens attributes performance differences to individual evaluated objects through comparisons under matched evaluation conditions, summarizing their performance and stability across multiple datasets and workloads. It further analyzes interactions among objects using analysis of variance (ANOVA), thereby turning vector database benchmarking from leaderboard ranking into a reproducible diagnostic procedure. VecDBLens reveals two key insights. First, retrieval performance is fundamentally upper-bounded by embedding quality, revealing that many apparent indexing failures are therefore representation-side limitations. Second, stronger index parameterization is not always beneficial: overly aggressive configurations can reshape neighborhood topology and cause recall regression. These results challenge the common practice of evaluating vector databases primarily through indexing and show that real-world limitations arise from constraints across retrieval functions rather than any single function alone. VecDBLens provides a principled basis for identifying the true performance drivers of vector databases and for guiding their design in practical applications.

### 8. CuEVM: Scaling Smart Contract Execution on GPUs

- arXiv ID: `2611.14916v1`
- Authors: Nhut-Minh Ho, Tien Tuan Anh Dinh, Quang Trung Ta, Fredrik Svantes, Xiaokui Xiao, Beng Chin Ooi
- Published: 2026-11-11
- Categories: `cs.DC`, `cs.CR`, `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14916v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14916v1)

Smart contracts on Ethereum blockchain process transactions worth billions of US dollars daily. They run on top of Ethereum Virtual Machine (EVM) which executes transactions sequentially. This execution model creates a performance bottleneck, especially for new blockchain workloads with large numbers of transactions, such as security testing. We present CuEVM, a GPU-native and semantically complete EVM execution engine that treats transaction execution as a massively parallel data processing problem. It employs novel data structures and GPU-aware scheduling to accelerate execution. Unlike prior works that transpile the contract and rely on a host CPU for many operations, CuEVM supports complete EVM semantics natively on the GPU. It passes 100% of the Ethereum VMTests and 96.2% of the full execution-test suite. On a single commodity GPU, CuEVM executes over 8 million ERC-20 transfers per second, outperforming existing baselines by up to two orders of magnitude. We build a smart contract fuzzer, called CuEVM-fuzz, on top of CuEVM. The fuzzer achieves over a million end-to-end executions per second, up to 23x and 4.8x higher than the state-of-the-art CPU-based and GPU-based baselines, respectively.

### 9. SynthCorp: A Platform for Creating Benchmarks for Data Agents

- arXiv ID: `2611.14765v1`
- Authors: Jiani Zhang, Manav Garg, Eugenie Lai, Cheng Zhen, Eva Sharma, Yang Pan, Yaming Lin, Alon Halevy
- Published: 2026-11-10
- Categories: `cs.AI`, `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14765v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14765v1)

Current benchmarks for LLM-driven data agents saturate quickly and cannot target specific capabilities. We present SynthCorp, a platform that creates customizable data estates and benchmarks by simulating how business events produce and modify corporate data over time. Each data estate combines relational databases, a document store, and a data catalog, paired with evaluation tasks and executable gold programs. First, given a scenario and target complexity dimensions, SynthCorp synthesizes reusable event types and compiles each into a program over predefined simulation primitives. Second, SynthCorp schedules event instances on a virtual calendar, interleaving routine and disruption events so that tables, documents, and catalog entries remain consistent. Third, SynthCorp executes the calendar to materialize the data estate and records every operation in a simulation log. Fourth, SynthCorp generates analytical tasks and derives verified gold programs from the log. We use SynthCorp to generate initial evaluation benchmarks comprising seven data estates (up to 1K operational tables) and 256 tasks across five domains. The data estates pass deterministic pipeline checks (such as foreign-key violations, undeclared writes, recurrence violations, and database execution failures) with a near-zero error rate, introduce cross-asset complexities, and cover all requested dimensions. Evaluating three models across four data-context settings shows that the benchmarks separate data contexts and model tiers. Auditing candidate queries against causal provenance in the simulation log verifies gold programs without manual annotation, including on multi-event composite tasks that expose reasoning failures in state-of-the-art agents. We hope the community uses SynthCorp to generate more challenging and useful data estates and benchmarks that foster the development of enterprise data agents.

### 10. Beyond Foreign-Key Connectivity: Semantic Relationship Representation for Relational Deep Learning

- arXiv ID: `2611.14706v1`
- Authors: Noy Cohen-Shapira
- Published: 2026-11-10
- Categories: `cs.DB`, `cs.LG`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14706v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14706v1)

Relational databases encode information through tables and primary--foreign key constraints. Although recent Relational Deep Learning (RDL) approaches represent these databases as heterogeneous graphs, a foreign-key connection primarily specifies structural connectivity between records. Its semantic interpretation may involve additional information that is not explicitly represented by connectivity alone.
  This paper introduces a conceptual framework for constructing relationship-aware representations from relational databases. The framework distinguishes three complementary aspects: (i) schema-defined relationship semantics, including endpoint types and foreign-key roles; (ii) association entities and explicitly available relationship-level attributes; and (iii) contextual information describing the temporal and structural circumstances of a connection.
  We formulate a Relationship Representation Construction (RRC) protocol that maps relational schema elements and database records to node representations, relationship descriptors, and contextual representations. A key principle of the protocol is semantic ownership: attributes are assigned to records, relationships, or context according to their meaning, rather than merely their physical location in a database table. Ambiguous attributes are not automatically promoted to relationship features.
  The resulting representations can be incorporated into existing relational graph learning architectures through relationship-conditioned message passing. The proposed framework is complementary to recent work on relational message passing, graph transformers, and task-dependent graph construction. We discuss its conceptual foundations, illustrate the construction protocol using a multi-table database, and identify research questions and empirical evaluations required to establish its potential benefits.

### 11. Where LLM Agents Get Stuck Debugging Databases: Access More Than Reasoning

- arXiv ID: `2611.17304v1`
- Authors: Gustavo Pinto
- Published: 2026-11-12
- Categories: `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17304v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17304v1)

Large language model (LLM) coding agents are increasingly asked not only to write code but to debug running systems from live runtime data. We report a field observation of one such agent (Claude Code) as it diagnosed eight database incidents in a production message-dispatch engine. In every incident whose root cause depended on runtime concurrency, the agent announced a cause, shipped a fix, and later walked it back, and each wrong guess cost a deploy-and-observe cycle. Mapping each incident to what the agent lacked, we find that access to production state was the most pervasive bottleneck. The missing access included live lock views, untruncated deadlock reports, read-only execution, and an explicit environment context. An access gap was present in all five concurrency-dependent incidents: alone in one, and in four combined with a reasoning error such as anchoring on an early cause, incomplete exploration of the configuration, or a misreading of the code's transaction semantics. The three incidents that did not depend on concurrency needed nothing beyond what the agent already had. Within the incidents, the agent's wrong hypotheses were consistent with the evidence it could see; they were not fabrications. We argue that the next step for production-debugging agents is a forensics layer that gives the agent the access it lacks inside existing security controls. Such a layer would combine a safe interrogation API, a lock-and-deadlock flight recorder, and a production replay harness.

### 12. Non-Functional Requirements to Hyperparameter Translation for Quantum Software via LLMs

- arXiv ID: `2611.16902v1`
- Authors: Joseph G. Bronyah, Nasir U. Eisty
- Published: 2026-11-12
- Categories: `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16902v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16902v1)

Quantum software engineering (QSE) lacks validated approaches for translating natural-language non-functional requirements (NFRs) into execution parameters such as shots and transpiler optimization levels. Existing methods emphasize semantic similarity rather than execution outcomes. We evaluate seven large language models (LLMs) against a deterministic heuristic baseline using 540 synthetic and 18 real-world user stories. We assess semantic accuracy and execution outcomes, including time, cost, top-state probability, and two-qubit gate exposure, through Qiskit/Aer simulations. All LLMs significantly outperformed the heuristic in semantic accuracy, achieving 55.6 to 83.3 percent on real-world stories compared to 16.7 percent for the heuristic. However, no LLM demonstrated statistically significant superiority over others on the real-world dataset. Speed- and cost-oriented configurations reduced execution time and cost, but fidelity improvements were not supported. Some failures resulted from provider safety refusals rather than translation errors. Our findings demonstrate the potential of LLMs as middleware for NFR-to-configuration translation in QSE, while emphasizing execution-based validation, human oversight, and careful interpretation of model differences.

### 13. CrossPloit: A Benchmark for Cross-Version PoC Migration in Python Web Applications

- arXiv ID: `2611.16588v1`
- Authors: Hung Nguyen-Huu, Nguyen Anh Tuan, Ting Zhang, Yue Liu, Chengran Yang, Lwin Khin Shar, Eng Lieh Ouh, David Lo
- Published: 2026-11-12
- Categories: `cs.CR`, `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16588v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16588v1)

Affected-version ranges in vulnerability databases decide which deployments scanners, patching pipelines, and incident responders treat as vulnerable, yet these ranges are often incomplete, so vulnerable releases are silently treated as safe. Auditing them requires executable evidence: given a proof-of-concept (PoC) exploit for a listed version, show that the same vulnerability can be triggered on an earlier, unlisted version. This cross-version PoC migration combines source-code analysis, environment repair, and execution-based verification. We introduce CrossPloit, a benchmark of 127 migration instances across 45 CVEs in the Python web ecosystem. Each instance includes a version-pinned container, a reference PoC, and a vulnerability-specific oracle; two validators check that the observed effect arises from the disclosed vulnerability. Benchmark construction confirms 149 vulnerable prior versions absent from the affected-version lists of four databases, 127 of which require migration. Across 18 agent configurations (nine models and two harnesses), the best configuration resolves 58.3% of instances, compared with 8.7% for an adapted trace-guided baseline. Attack-vector shifts are particularly difficult: no configuration resolves more than 37.9% of these instances. Higher resolve rates are associated with a smaller share of missed-API failures and a larger share of incomplete executions among the remaining failures. In one configuration, withholding the reference PoC and its environment specification reduces resolve rate from 50.4% to 15.0%, highlighting the value of the supplied reference package. CrossPloit provides an execution-based measurement of under-reported exposure and of how far its enumeration can be automated.

### 14. Guided Generation of Linux Kernel Semantic Patches with Small Local Language Models

- arXiv ID: `2611.16215v1`
- Authors: Mohanned Ahmed
- Published: 2026-11-12
- Categories: `cs.SE`, `cs.AI`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16215v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16215v1)

When a Linux kernel API changes, hundreds of drivers are converted by hand over months, and the earlier conversions are reviewed examples for the later ones. We ask whether small open language models running on a laptop can turn such examples into Coccinelle semantic patches, the rules kernel maintainers already use for tree-wide changes. We adapt guided code generation to this task: the training examples are decomposed by script into a tree of change variants, the model writes one small rule per variant under feedback from applying it to the examples, deterministic components repair SmPL syntax and generalise driver-specific names, rules are composed greedily, and a compile critic repairs or rejects a rule whose output does not build. We evaluate on a pre-registered test set of 7 migrations whose new APIs appeared after the training cutoffs of all three models (Qwen2.5-Coder 7B, Llama 3.1 8B, Gemma 3 4B), with 122 held-out driver conversions, counting a conversion as solved only if the rule's output matches the human fix and compiles. With the framework, Qwen2.5-Coder solves 35.3 conversions on average over three seeds (range 32-41), against 1.0 when prompted directly; Spinfer solves 41 and a frontier LLM agent 66. The local model solves fewer conversions than Spinfer but its rules are the most precise on the test set (0.74 of the files they change are solved, against 0.55 for Spinfer and 0.70 for the frontier agent), and the compile critic removes every output that matches the human fix but does not build. The two general-purpose models stay weak even with guidance (at most 12 solved). All code, prompts, model responses and results are released.

### 15. Spec-Driven PBT: Deriving Semantic Invariants from Code Specifications for Agentic Property-based Testing

- arXiv ID: `2611.15920v1`
- Authors: Aditya Shukla, Kartik Gupta, Ali Alavi, Pashootan Vaezipoor
- Published: 2026-11-11
- Categories: `cs.SE`, `cs.AI`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15920v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15920v1)

Software is increasingly written by AI agents that produce an implementation from a natural language specification, a practice known as agentic coding. Code is now produced faster than it can be reviewed, and has been shown to exhibit its own characteristic failure patterns. Property-based testing (PBT), a long-standing technique in which invariant properties of a program are checked against many generated inputs, has recently been revisited with LLM agents authoring the properties, thereby removing the high-effort derivation of invariants that has long hindered its adoption. Existing methods, however, derive these properties from the implementation alone (Code-driven PBT). At the same time, a thorough specification such as a product requirements document has become routine within agentic coding, yet its role in testing the resulting code remains underexplored. We study the value of the specification as a source of properties for testing agent-written code. Our key insight is that the specification, unlike the implementation, records what the code was meant to do, and invariants derived from it are not biased by particular implementation choices. We propose Spec-Driven PBT, an agent that first expresses invariants in natural language from the specification and only then inspects the implementation to translate them into concrete tests. Across 25 applications from ViBench, Spec-Driven PBT finds a novel set of bugs that Code-driven PBT misses, while writing 2.4x fewer property tests. Though the two approaches surface a roughly equivalent number of bugs, there is little overlap: nearly 60% of the 169 total bugs are found by only one. Qualitatively, we find that the bugs unique to Spec-Driven PBT tend to violate documented product workflows whereas those to Code-driven PBT instead concentrate on implementation robustness, offering complementary value for bug discovery in agent-written code.

### 16. Bug Finding With Agentic Symbolic Execution

- arXiv ID: `2611.15596v1`
- Authors: George Granberry, Wolfgang Ahrendt, Andy Gordon, Moa Johansson
- Published: 2026-11-11
- Categories: `cs.SE`, `cs.AI`, `cs.LO`, `cs.SC`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15596v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15596v1)

Symbolic execution, in tools such as CrossHair for Python, is a powerful technique for finding bugs, but two barriers hinder its adoption: the human effort of annotating code with assertions, and the need to model the execution environment outside Python, such as an external database. To overcome both, we design an agentic harness that orchestrates the symbolic execution tool CrossHair together with an AI coding agent. Without human intervention, it instruments a repository with assertions, synthesizes a constraint-aware symbolic environment for the database, runs symbolic execution, and constructs a concrete test as evidence for each bug it reports. We evaluate our harness on 1,156,044 lines of Python code over 11 popular open-source Python web applications (Zulip, NetBox, Apache Superset, and others). With the synthesized environment, CrossHair executes 81% of the 5,406 contracted functions, against 4.5% without it. Our pipeline finds 1,050 bugs, each reproduced by a test against the unmodified application, 221 of them by CrossHair, and 312 of them are missed by a baseline in which the same LLM inspects the code. The two find different kinds of bugs: CrossHair's are mostly crashes on input-domain corner cases that validation admits, such as empty strings, missing fields and unusual characters, while inspection's are mostly bugs that produce wrong output. Our framework thus lets symbolic execution supplement inspection-based bug finding.

### 17. KernelAudit: Contract-Aware Auditing of Validators for LLM-Generated GPU Kernels

- arXiv ID: `2611.15506v1`
- Authors: Ziyuan Li, Zihe Song, Xiaofang Qi, Wei Yang, Tao Xie
- Published: 2026-11-11
- Categories: `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15506v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15506v1)

The effectiveness of generating GPU kernels driven by a large language model (LLM) is highly dependent on the quality of the used validator (used to accept or reject a generated kernel candidate against the contracts of the given kernel-generation task), whose results inform reward assignment, candidate selection, and speedup claims during kernel generation. However, an existing validator suffers from various limitations, e.g., wrongly accepting a candidate due to missing its forbidden library use or wrongly rejecting a candidate due to determining its permitted library use as forbidden (constraints on library uses are stated in the task contracts).
  To assess the quality of a validator against the given task contracts, we propose an independent-audit approach for the validator by (1) construction of audit oracle: labeling a candidate as PASS or FAIL based on three separate dimensions in our proposed contract model (i.e., semantic correctness, implementation integrity, and measurement integrity) and (2) auditing of the validator against the audit oracle: comparing the labels of the candidate against the validation results produced by the validator. For semantic correctness, reference-calibrated stress tests check output correctness on valid inputs for a candidate. For implementation integrity, reference and candidate execution traces are used to determine specific library uses. For measurement integrity, timing checks assess whether the reported runtime reflects the full time needed to complete the candidate's computation. We apply our approach to assess the quality of six validators and 716 candidates from seven corpora, including constructed controls. The evaluation results show that our approach helps assess the existing six validators to have nontrivial false-reject rates. There is high-degree agreement between the candidate labels produced by our audit oracle and human judgments.

### 18. EvoX: Rethinking the Agent Harness as a Runtime Policy

- arXiv ID: `2611.15006v1`
- Authors: Haoyang Zhang, Qumeng Sun, Xiao Zhang, Xiang Liu, Zhidong Fan, Junjie Wang
- Published: 2026-11-11
- Categories: `cs.AI`, `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15006v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15006v1)

Large language model (LLM) agents increasingly interact with tools and environments, yet plausible model decisions do not always translate into successful execution due to tool-call mismatches, interrupted workflows, and unsupported completion claims. We present EvoX, a Rust-based, multi-model agent runtime guided by The Second Policy, which views the harness as an Operational Policy complementing the foundation model's Semantic Policy. EvoX combines model-facing guidance with deterministic runtime control through three complementary mechanisms: Action Realization, Execution Continuity, and Outcome Grounding. To evaluate harnesses without suppressing their native behaviors, we introduce Contrast, a controlled and auditable framework integrating explicit fairness constraints, provider-bound request verification, and evidence-based adjudication. Across five benchmarks, six foundation models, and six agent harnesses, EvoX achieves the highest numerical success rate of 56.2% over 691 common task--model pairs, exceeding the average of the other five harnesses by approximately 3.0 percentage points. Its larger advantages on GLM-5.3 (+10.7 points) and Kimi K3 (+7.9 points) highlight the model-dependent effectiveness of harness design and the potential for cross-provider adaptability. Achieved without memory, evolution, or extensions, these results establish EvoX as a competitive multi-model agent runtime and underscore the importance of harness-level operational control in realizing foundation-model capabilities.
