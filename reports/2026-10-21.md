### 1. NousDB - A Fast Query Engine for Semantic Analytics

- arXiv ID: `2610.28551v1`
- Authors: Christos Tsapelas, Georgia Koutrika, Yannis Ioannidis
- Published: 2026-10-20
- Categories: `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28551v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28551v1)

Semantic data analytics systems integrate AI-powered operators into query pipelines, enabling declarative queries over structured and unstructured data. However, existing systems rely on per-tuple model invocations, ad hoc operator designs, and lack principled cost models, resulting in poor scalability and limited optimization. We present NousDB, a high-performance semantic analytics engine built on Apache DataFusion\cite{10.1145/3626246.3653368}.Through NousDB we propose an extended relational algebra with three semantic operators and formal algebraic properties that enable principled query rewriting and cross-operator optimization. We further propose a token-based cost model using equi-depth token histograms to optimize batch sizing under context-window constraints and provide statistical guarantees for safe batching. Experiments show that NousDB achieves up to $\times 86$ speedups with both local and enterprise LLMs, while leading competitors in token efficiency and accuracy. These gains extend to complex JOB and Stack workloads augmented with semantic operators, supporting both traditional and semantic analytics.

### 2. SWatcher+: A Holistic Approach to Deploying Learned Cardinality Estimators in a Dynamic Setting

- arXiv ID: `2610.28270v1`
- Authors: Yuxi Liu, Vincent Capol, Xiao Hu, Pankaj K. Agarwal, Jun Yang
- Published: 2026-10-20
- Categories: `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28270v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28270v1)

Cardinality estimation (CE) is crucial in database query optimization. Although recent advances have improved accuracy, deploying machine-learned CE models in dynamic databases remains challenging due to data updates and workload shifts. Retraining can be costly, but existing practices offer little guidance on when and how to update models, resulting in simple periodic retaining strategies that are suboptimal. We propose SWatcher+, a framework that adapts learned CE to dynamic workloads by monitoring data updates and query feedback. SWatcher+ selectively monitors high-impact subqueries for which wrong estimates lead to poor plan performance, and it proactively applies corrections to estimates during query optimization. This strategy enables immediate adaption to data and workload shifts without waiting for full model retraining, and carefully balances the cost and benefit of monitoring and adaption versus retraining. Our experiments show that SWatcher+ offers a better trade-off between query performance and overhead compared with existing approaches, making it a more practical solution for deploying learned CE in dynamic settings.

### 3. Containment of Conjunctive Queries with Negative Information over Semirings

- arXiv ID: `2610.27957v1`
- Authors: Guillermo Badia, Carles Noguera, Gaia Petreni, Alice Portone, Val Tannen
- Published: 2026-10-20
- Categories: `cs.LO`, `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27957v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27957v1)

We study the containment problem for (unions of) conjunctive queries ((U)CQs) with negative information on semiring-annotated databases. Our approach to negative information follows the treatment of negation proposed by Grädel and Tannen (2017, 2019, 2021 and 2024) for first-order logic and fixed point logic which applies productively to large classes of semirings, including bounded distributive lattices, as well as the important provenance semirings studied for positive CQ containment by Green (2009). Negation features in (U)CQs along two dimensions: negated equalities (disequations), and negated relational atoms. We study complexity for each of them separately, as well as jointly. For disequations, we extend a methodology that proves the same complexity bounds as the ones already known for the standard Boolean semiring (in the second level of the polynomial hierarchy) for most semirings of interest. Two prominent exceptions are $\mathbb{N}[X]$ and Trio(X) where the complexity bound for containment is in the second level of the counting hierarchy. For CQs with negated relational atoms we show that the complexity, surprisingly, is the same as that of the containment of CQs without negation when one considers model-defining and provenance-tracking interpretations (namely, nondeterministic polynomial time). Interestingly, among bounded distributive lattices, the Boolean two-element case is the only one where the complexity of the problem continues to be hard for the second level of the polynomial hierarchy for model-defining interpretations. Furthermore, we show that the complexity increases again for distributive lattices if we focus on other classes of interpretations. Finally, we consider queries that have both negated equalities and negated relational atoms and show that the complexity of containment continues to be that of the case with just negated equalities.

### 4. MultiCard: Multiple Attribute Cardinality Estimation via Deep Learning

- arXiv ID: `2610.27019v1`
- Authors: Mobina Koochaknia, Maryam Ramezani, Mohammad Hasan Shafaat
- Published: 2026-10-19
- Categories: `cs.DB`, `cs.IR`, `cs.LG`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27019v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27019v1)

Cardinality estimation is essential for query optimization, but most existing learned estimators focus on a single predicate type, such as string LIKE clauses or numerical range filters. In practice, analytical workloads often combine both types, while the accuracy of string estimators tends to decrease with longer patterns. We introduce MultiCard, a comprehensive learned cardinality estimator that unifies LIKE predicates and numerical filters within a single end-to-end architecture. By combining a Transformer for long-range sparse patterns with an LSTM for short, frequent patterns, MultiCard achieves robust pattern modeling. In parallel, a numerical encoder integrates catalog histograms and structural predicate features, enabling the model to dynamically assess histogram estimation accuracy across different operators and data skew conditions. A final Transformer layer captures interactions among heterogeneous predicates before a multilayer perceptron predicts query cardinalities. We also introduce UPCI, a workload generator that produces length-balanced LIKE patterns, multi-column numerical predicates, and exact cardinality labels for training and evaluation. Experiments on five real-world datasets show that, for string-only workloads, MultiCard achieves the best or near-best Q-error across all datasets compared with state-of-the-art methods. For mixed multi-attribute queries, MultiCard reduces PostgreSQL's mean Q-error by up to 16 times while maintaining an inference latency below 5 ms.

### 5. VS-Flash: Harnessing Vector Search with Computational Memory-Semantic Flash

- arXiv ID: `2610.26960v1`
- Authors: Donguk Kim, Sangjun Pae, Daeyoung Lee, Dong Kim
- Published: 2026-10-18
- Categories: `cs.DB`, `cs.DC`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26960v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26960v1)

As generative AI continues to evolve, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems are driving rapid growth in vector database capacity requirements. As datasets outgrow host DRAM, vector databases rely on MMap-based serving over SSDs to extend capacity. However, high-fan-out ANN searches generate concurrent irregular accesses that contend for host memory and storage resources, degrading bulk throughput and inflating tail latency under mixed workloads. In this paper, we address these challenges through memory-semantic capacity and workload-aware compute placement. VS-Flash exposes flash as a large-capacity memory-semantic space and dynamically selects the execution engine according to runtime query and workload characteristics. It offloads throughput-oriented large-batch queries to a Computational Memory-Semantic Flash (CMS-Flash) device, while executing latency-sensitive small-batch queries on the host CPU. Both paths access the same resident segments, avoiding a separate host-DRAM replica. Across three billion-scale datasets, VS-Flash improves bulk-search throughput over vanilla Milvus by 36.8X on average, with up to a 57.4X improvement. Under mixed workloads, VS-Flash reduces the p99 latency of latency-sensitive requests by up to 24.8X while sustaining device-side bulk search. These results show that VS-Flash delivers scalable vector search beyond host DRAM capacity while jointly improving bulk throughput and small-request tail latency.

### 6. Industrial ReMEmbR: Scaling Spatiotemporal Memory to Robot Fleets

- arXiv ID: `2610.28624v1`
- Authors: Jad Yammine, Malek Wahidi, Abrar Anwar, Yan Chang, Mihir Acharya, Anthony Rizk
- Published: 2026-10-20
- Categories: `cs.RO`, `cs.AI`, `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28624v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28624v1)

Warehouse robot fleets continuously observe operational conditions, but preserving these observations as reusable semantic memory incurs substantial computation, communication, and storage costs. For example, explaining recurring aisle blockages can require reviewing video from multiple robots across several shifts. We present IndustrialRemembr, a shared spatiotemporal memory system that extends NVIDIA ReMEmbR to robot fleets and reduces server-side computation by generating scene descriptions only on demand. On each robot, an NVIDIA Jetson encodes video clips into retrieval embeddings with Cosmos-Embed1, vision tokens with Cosmos3-Edge, and reconstruction latents with the Wan2.2 VAE, without uploading raw camera frames. The encoding models are re-used to detect people in image patches, zeroing them out to support PII abstraction for GDPR-compliant deployments. We split Cosmos3-Edge at its vision-language boundary, placing the vision encoder on the robot and the language decoder on an on-premises server. A Nemotron agent retrieves memories by content, position, and time and queries the decoder using cached vision tokens. This design eliminates caption generation at write time, while cached-token decoding reproduces the unsplit model's output in all 450 tested decodes.

### 7. TraceLite: Efficient Trace Reduction for SWE Agents via Optical Compression and Redundancy Removal

- arXiv ID: `2610.28612v1`
- Authors: Jingyu Xiao, Qianxi Liu, Yintong Huo, Yuan Jiang, Michael R. Lyu
- Published: 2026-10-20
- Categories: `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28612v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28612v1)

Software engineering agents solve repository-level tasks through iterative tool use, but their continuously growing interaction traces incur substantial token consumption and inference overhead. Existing compression approaches often require model training, auxiliary LLM calls, or assumptions about specific content such as source code. We present TraceLite, a simple yet effective, training-free framework for compressing heterogeneous SWE-agent observations. Our preliminary study shows that optical rendering preserves substantial textual and semantic information under moderate compression, while agent trajectories exhibit both cross-step and phase-level redundancy. Motivated by these findings, TraceLite preserves recent observations as high-fidelity text, renders historical observations as compact images, removes overlapping content, and discards phase-expired observation payloads after code editing. It requires neither offline training nor auxiliary LLM inference. We evaluate TraceLite on SWE-bench Verified and DeepSWE using GPT-5.6-Luna and Gemini-3.8Flash as backbone models. On SWE-bench Verified, TraceLite achieves 78.25% resolve rate, outperforming the evaluated compression baselines while reducing average input-token consumption and API cost by 33.05% and 21.30%, respectively. Moreover, it introduces only 0.438 seconds of compression latency per observation. These results demonstrate that TraceLite improves SWE-agent efficiency with low processing overhead and without compromising issue-resolution capability. Our code is available at https://github.com/xjywhu/TraceLite.

### 8. Foreword: Pre-Generation Prediction and Characterisation of LLM Test Failures using the Jacobian Lens

- arXiv ID: `2610.28187v1`
- Authors: Somin Kim, Robert Feldt, Shin Yoo
- Published: 2026-10-20
- Categories: `cs.SE`, `cs.LG`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28187v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28187v1)

Testing LLM-based software is expensive: each input needs several sampled responses, each judged by a costly oracle. Further, every prompt or model change calls for re-testing. Pre-generation adequacy measures cut this cost by ranking inputs by failure risk based on the model's internal state. However, the state of the art, Clotho, models only passing reference inputs, and its hidden-state representation says nothing about why inputs fail. We present Foreword, a framework built on two ideas: learn from both passing and failing references, and represent inputs through the Jacobian lens (J-lens), an interpretability technique that reads intermediate activations out as scores over vocabulary tokens. Foreword pairs these representations with two estimators: a sparse logistic-regression probe or dual Gaussian Mixture Models (dual GMMs), which also yield a reference density (to flag inputs the references do not cover) and failing-class components (to group similar failures). On ten tasks and three open-weight LLMs, all four combinations of representations and estimators outperform Clotho on average, both for prediction of failure (ROC-AUC improves by 0.14 on average and by up to 0.31) and test prioritisation (15% more failures among the 100 top-ranked inputs on average, up to 33%). Prioritisation obtained using small models also transfers better to proprietary models (on average 22% and up to 41% more failures in the first quarter of prioritised inputs). Importantly, our qualitative case study shows that Foreword supports semantic characterisation of failures by pointing to ambiguities in labels or task specifications. Combined, Foreword can support testers for prioritising inputs, checking support for a prediction, and characterising specific failures, all without generating output.

### 9. Hallucinations of Large Language Models in Cross-Language Code Translation: Phenomena and Mitigation

- arXiv ID: `2610.27868v1`
- Authors: Yali Du, Yunji Zhang, Ming Li
- Published: 2026-10-20
- Categories: `cs.SE`, `cs.AI`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27868v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27868v1)

Code translation converts source code from one programming language to another while preserving its functionality. Although Large Language Models (LLMs) have improved code translation, their outputs still contain hallucinations: generated code that conflicts with the source program, violates target-language facts, or introduces contextually inconsistent dependencies. These failures are especially harmful in translation because small semantic deviations can silently change program behavior. Existing hallucination studies mainly focus on natural-language generation or code generation, leaving the forms, distribution, and mitigation of hallucinations in cross-language code translation insufficiently characterized. This paper presents a systematic empirical study of LLM hallucinations in code translation. Across three benchmark datasets, six language pairs, and seven LLMs, we construct a taxonomy with three major categories: Semantic Consistency Conflicts, Factual Knowledge Conflicts, and Project Context Deviations. We manually annotate 13,919 hallucination cases and analyze how hallucination types vary across models, datasets, and language directions. Guided by the taxonomy, we further propose HaluCodeTran, a mitigation framework that combines BM25-based retrieval of relevant translation examples with taxonomy-aware reflective refinement. The study provides an execution-tested characterization of code-translation hallucinations and a practical baseline for building more reliable LLM-based code migration tools.

### 10. Value-level data provenance in an electronic laboratory notebook used by humans and language-model agents: An experience report

- arXiv ID: `2610.27859v1`
- Authors: Oleksiy V. Penkov, Boyang Shen
- Published: 2026-10-20
- Categories: `physics.app-ph`, `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27859v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27859v1)

A published laboratory number can be verified only when its raw file and extraction method are known. This paper presents the value-level provenance architecture of APS ELN, an electronic laboratory notebook used by both researchers and language-model agents. The architecture was motivated by a production audit in a materials laboratory covering projects performed over almost 5 years. Tracing eight baseline studies from sample to stored value showed that the initial ELN structure preserved the link from sample to raw file. Still, the link from raw file to stored value was lost, mainly due to human factors: manual entries carried no file reference or extractor version. To close this gap, the database design and workflow were reworked so that every stored value records its source file, the data file reader/viewer (DFR) plugin and version, any interactive analysis selection, or the server-side formula for derived quantities. An automated drift check re-runs each DFR on the stored files. In two end-to-end validation studies recorded by agents in X-ray reflectivity and tribology, all stored values retained verifiable provenance with zero drift. A comparison with five other platforms shows that none binds individual stored numbers to both their source files and extractor versions.

### 11. Raise or Check? Predicting the Necessity of Additional Fuzzing for Continuous Fuzzing

- arXiv ID: `2610.27828v1`
- Authors: Riku Kato, Tatsuya Shirai, Olivier Nourry, Yutaro Kashiwa, Kenji Fujiwara, Yasutaka Kamei, Hajimu Iida
- Published: 2026-10-20
- Categories: `cs.SE`, `cs.CR`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27828v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27828v1)

Continuous fuzzing is used for vulnerability detection in Open Source Software (OSS). Large-scale services such as Google's OSS-Fuzz fuzz over 1,300 projects and have revealed more than 10,000 vulnerabilities. Despite this, a considerable time lag often exists between when a vulnerability is introduced and when it is detected (i.e., the window of exposure). Given the computational cost of additional fuzzing, we aim to allocate fuzzing resources efficiently using vulnerability-risk estimates. We propose RAISE (Risk-Aware Intensive Supplemental Execution Framework), a Just-In-Time (JIT) framework that prioritizes additional fuzzing for high-risk project-days. RAISE performs binary classification at the project-day granularity to estimate whether a vulnerability is introduced on a given day. We construct a dataset combining vulnerability-introducing commits from the OSV database with daily coverage and change metrics from OSS-Fuzz, covering 15 C/C++ projects and 15,210 project-days, of which 347 (2.28%) are vulnerability-introducing. In a cross-project validation setting, our best model, a Random Forest combining VCCFinder-based static features with dynamic fuzzing features, achieves a ROC-AUC of 0.74, substantially outperforming a random baseline; coverage metrics, led by PatchCoverage, are the most important features. In a simulation over 968 vulnerabilities comparing five allocation strategies, Simple-Regression was significantly more cost-efficient than the prediction-agnostic heuristics, spending 91.60 additional fuzzing sessions per detected vulnerability, roughly half the cost of the Median heuristic (171.89). RAISE shows that JIT vulnerability prediction with coverage-aware fuzzing features can effectively guide risk-aware allocation of continuous fuzzing resources.

### 12. MIMOS-Tools: An Integrated Toolchain for Model-Based Design of Embedded Systems

- arXiv ID: `2610.27826v1`
- Authors: Wang Yi, Chengzi Huang, Behnam Khodabandeloo, Duc Anh Nguyen
- Published: 2026-10-20
- Categories: `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27826v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27826v1)

Despite decades of progress in model-based methods and tools for embedded real-time systems, there is still no integrated environment that supports the entire development lifecycle. This paper presents MIMOS-Tools, a comprehensive toolchain spanning the key phases of development, including modeling, simulation, formal verification, real-time scheduling, schedulability and end-to-end latency analysis, and code generation for heterogeneous multicore platforms. All phases are grounded in a single, coherent, and deterministic model of computation, the MIMOS model, for which we present a formal operational semantics and establish both functional and timing determinism. This semantic foundation ensures that properties verified at the model level are preserved throughout the development process and in the generated implementation.
  A distinguishing feature of MIMOS-Tools is its support for not only deterministic but also modular, compositional design. Unlike synchronous design environments such as SCADE and Simulink, which rely on a global base clock and offline static scheduling, MIMOS-Tools supports time-triggered asynchronous communication together with flexible real-time scheduling of computation. This removes the tight global coupling of the synchronous paradigm: components can be added, modified, or updated without recomputing a system-wide schedule, enabling scalable and incremental design and dynamic updates throughout the system lifecycle. However, MIMOS-Tools also supports Simulink models when the deadlines of tasks are set to zero to emulate the zero-time semantics of synchronous design.
  The toolchain is available at uu-mimos.github.io.
