### 1. Tessera: Fast Join-Aggregates over Skewed Sparse Relations in Database Engines

- arXiv ID: `2610.40620v1`
- Authors: Gyuyeong Kim
- Published: 2026-10-29
- Categories: `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40620v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40620v1)

Join-aggregate queries count or sum matching rows for each pair of values, often over skewed sparse relations in which a few values are frequent and most pairs of values never co-occur. Conventional execution plans materialize billions of intermediate join rows, taking minutes to tens of minutes per query. Recent matrix-based solutions reformulate these queries as matrix multiplication to reduce or avoid this materialization. However, they remain slow because they either store result cells for non-matching pairs or still materialize intermediate join rows for infrequent values. We present Tessera, a skew-aware join-aggregate operator for database engines that avoids both sources of overhead. Specifically, Tessera computes a sparse matrix product directly over the engine's rows, emits only nonzero cells as result rows, and prunes values that cannot meet the query's threshold before multiplication begins. It also offloads the dense block formed by pairs of the most frequent values to a CPU matrix accelerator when that block is dense enough. Experimental results show that Tessera is faster than the fastest existing system by 2.8x to 18.8x. In addition, Tessera outperforms DuckDB's execution plans by up to 1,802x.

### 2. Rebuilding a Field's Open-Problem Catalog from the Literature: A Two-Layer Knowledge Graph, and Why Status Must Stay Proposed

- arXiv ID: `2610.39641v1`
- Authors: Madhulatha Mandarapu, Sandeep Kunkunuru
- Published: 2026-10-29
- Categories: `cs.DB`, `cs.AI`, `cs.DL`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39641v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39641v1)

A research field records its open problems in surveys, keynotes and community reports. Those are prose, written every few years, and nobody can ask them who works on a problem now or whether it was settled last year. We argue the problems should be data, and we build the system that makes them so: a hand-curated problem layer carried in unchanged, under an evidence layer rebuilt from a bibliographic index, joined by typed, provenance-carrying links in one graph database and queryable from one language. A language model does four jobs -- per-paper extraction, catalog grounding, per-paper status verdict, and idea generation -- each constrained to a candidate set so out-of-set answers are refused rather than stored. We report a study on two curated catalogs, database research (1,053 problems) and AI research (1,000 problems), each with a prior catalog-only graph. The rebuilt graph answers every question the catalog-only graph answered -- 47 of 47 on databases, 34 of 35 on AI -- plus questions the catalog-only graph refused: recent workers, untouched problems, methods tried, and outside papers bearing on a problem (336 problems, 770 works on databases). The capability we most wanted does not survive its own audit. Of 41 solves and refutes verdicts hand-checked against the papers, 9 are correct, 30 partly correct and 2 wrong; no refutes verdict (0 of 34) disproves an open problem, and only 1 of 7 solves closes the central question. We report that failure in full and draw the design conclusion: a status change must be proposed with its evidence and never applied, making the human reviewer part of the architecture. We close with the data-engineering problems this raises: entity resolution across curated and harvested layers, verifying model status claims, freshness under source quotas, and evaluation without ground truth.

### 3. Efficient Group-Penalty k-means for Text Clustering with Noisy LLM-Generated Constraints

- arXiv ID: `2610.38605v1`
- Authors: Feiteng Nie, Yuan Sun, Shengkun Zhu, Sheng Wang, Zhiyong Peng
- Published: 2026-10-28
- Categories: `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38605v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38605v1)

k-means provides a simple and efficient foundation for text clustering, but embedding alone can miss task-specific semantic boundaries. Must-link (ML) and cannot-link (CL) constraints can correct such errors, yet conventional constrained clustering assumes that these relations are already supplied. LLMs can generate them without task-specific labels, but exhaustive querying is costly and their judgments can be incorrect. We present GPKmeans, an efficient Group-Penalty k-means method for text clustering with noisy LLM-generated constraints. GPKmeans uses LLM as an offline local pairwise annotator, querying selected anchor-candidate pairs for sparse ML links and constructing set-valued CL evidence without all-pairs queries. It converts connected components of the ML-link graph and the generated CL sets into groups with normalized, bounded soft penalties, limiting each group's influence independently of its size. Cached coordinate descent is proposed to efficiently solve the constraints clustering problem. We prove finite-step convergence and derive deterministic and probabilistic oracle-referenced bounds that relate objective distortion to the penalty coefficients and to the group-level error probabilities of the generated constraints. Across nine datasets, GPKmeans uses fewer LLM calls and yields more accurate constraints. It achieves the best average ACC, NMI, and ARI while running approximately 30 times faster than state-of-the-art constrained clustering methods.

### 4. CoWalk: Cooperative Walks for Agentic Batch Queries on Disk-Resident Graphs

- arXiv ID: `2610.38404v1`
- Authors: Qiao Liang, Zhiguang Chen, Yue Yu, Nong Xiao
- Published: 2026-10-28
- Categories: `cs.DB`, `cs.IR`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38404v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38404v1)

Workloads such as query rewriting, deep research and group-relative policy optimization (GRPO) issue batches of semantically related vector queries. Existing disk-resident approximate nearest neighbor (ANN) systems process these queries independently. On three workloads, 31 to 67 percent of the I/Os of a batch fetch a page that another query of the batch has already fetched. A page cache shared by the batch removes these I/Os, yet it makes a single-thread search only $1.1$ to $1.2{\times}$ faster, because the search time goes to distance computation and the cache does not change the walk of any query. We present CoWalk, which runs the queries of a batch as cooperating walks on one thread. Every query keeps its own candidate list, a page is read once per batch, and the full-precision vector on every fetched page is ranked by every query of the batch. A query therefore reaches the target recall with a shorter candidate list, which computes fewer distances and issues fewer I/Os. At recall@10 of 0.95, CoWalk answers a batch on one thread $1.6$ to $1.9{\times}$ faster than the fastest per-query baseline and issues $2.8$ to $6.9{\times}$ fewer I/Os. On a server it serves $3.0$ to $6.9{\times}$ the throughput of that baseline at a similar batch latency.

### 5. LASSO: An LLM-Agent Synthesized System for SQL Validation

- arXiv ID: `2610.38294v1`
- Authors: Shreyas Jadhav, S. Sudarshan
- Published: 2026-10-28
- Categories: `cs.DB`, `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38294v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38294v1)

The rise of Large Language Models (LLMs) has significantly advanced text-to-SQL capabilities, but generated queries may have errors. Test datasets are important for catching errors, but having only one or a few test datasets leaves many bugs undetected. Effective testing requires multiple test datasets that can expose subtle semantic bugs and edge cases. Recent work on test data generation and equivalence checking has focused on using constraint solvers. However, constraint-based approaches are slow and not scalable. In this paper, we introduce LASSO, a framework inspired by XData, but designed to minimize the use of constraint solvers, instead prioritizing use of data generation strategies based on fast heuristics. LASSO deploys a number of "attack strategies", each targeting a specific class of semantic errors. LASSO code was built using LLM-based agentic coding. We used carefully designed workflows and rules to support effective code generation and to avoid common code generation issues. A large number of internal test cases are included in LASSO to catch errors in generated code, and more are added when we extend LASSO, to avoid subsequent regressions. Our empirical evaluation across multiple benchmarks demonstrates that LASSO outperforms the state of the art, including XData, SpotIT, ParSEval, and Polygon.

### 6. Revision-Bound Evidence Cuts: Authenticated Queries and Contextual Witness Capacity

- arXiv ID: `2610.37982v1`
- Authors: Faruk Alpay, Taylan Alpay
- Published: 2026-10-27
- Categories: `cs.CR`, `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37982v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37982v1)

Authentic records can support unauthorized narratives when their qualifications, query scope, or counterevidence are silently dropped. We present revision-bound evidence cuts, a framework for authenticating both supporting and opposing inference obligations across changing source versions. Signed complete-update frontiers permit transport of unaffected certificates, alternative-cut recovery, and an exact certified-reuse radius given by a frontier-hypergraph transversal number. Their source obligations induce an integral model of context-bound authorization. To secure database-derived claims, we introduce an authenticated relational query contract that binds original source records, complete selected bags, counting units, parameters, and aggregates independently of execution strategy. A full-source verifier checks 16,824 Chogha Mish records across SQLite scans, indexes, and materialized views; three valid strategies agree and eight contract/view mutations are rejected. A separate 340-row NISP cohort shows that changing the admitted weighting rule can reverse a comparison while preserving every source record. A frozen English text-to-frame compiler produces qualified evidence receipts, with linguistic accuracy evaluated separately from cryptographic integrity. We also separate logical-source from transport commitments so lossless re-encoding need not invalidate unchanged scientific evidence. The SCENE prototype evaluates signed revisions, authenticated queries, scoped disclosures, and adversarial mutations at scales reaching one million source records. These guarantees rely on declared policies and independently pinned trust roots; they authenticate specified computations and obligations, not historical truth, complete counterevidence, or institutional endorsement.

### 7. Lost in the Latent Space: How Code Embeddings Represent Functional Intent and Implementation Fidelity

- arXiv ID: `2610.39519v1`
- Authors: Lu Sun, Yuheng Huang, Lei Ma, Zhijie Wang
- Published: 2026-10-29
- Categories: `cs.SE`, `cs.CL`, `cs.IR`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39519v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39519v1)

Code embedding models have become a key building block for many software engineering applications, yet their internal representations remain poorly understood. Existing evaluations tend to focus on downstream task performance, offering limited insight into whether a single embedding can support both functional abstraction and implementation-sensitive analysis. To address this gap, we present a large-scale empirical study of 14 state-of-the-art code embedding models. We evaluate their representational behavior through three complementary analyses: supervised probing for functional abstraction and implementation fidelity, zero-shot retrieval experiments to assess geometric similarity, and controlled perturbation experiments that measure embedding responses to semantics-preserving code transformations. The results reveal a consistent abstraction-fidelity tension: models that better preserve stylistic and structural signals tend to do so at the expense of semantic generalization, while models with similar retrieval performance can nevertheless respond very differently to the same implementation changes. Overall, our findings suggest that a single universal code representation remains out of reach, motivating more representation-aware evaluation, selection, and deployment of code embedding models in software engineering.

### 8. When Relevant Code Is Wrong: Runtime-Aware Context for AI Coding Agents

- arXiv ID: `2610.38800v1`
- Authors: Wesley K. G. Assunção, Ramesh Periyathambi
- Published: 2026-10-28
- Categories: `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38800v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38800v1)

AI coding agents increasingly rely on retrieved code and development history to guide software changes. However, highly relevant artifacts may describe implementations that are inactive or incompatible with the target deployment. Neither semantic similarity nor recency establishes whether retrieved information remains applicable under feature flags, architectural migrations, and deployment-specific configurations. We challenge relevance-oriented retrieval by introducing operational context validity, a task-, deployment-, and time-dependent criterion for determining whether software evidence can legitimately guide implementation decisions. We instantiate this new retrieval principle in CASE, an agent-agnostic framework that connects repository artifacts with runtime configurations and architectural evolution, classifies evidence as valid, invalid, or unknown, and generates context packages distinguishing actionable guidance from historical rationale and unresolved constraints. We investigate its feasibility through a reproducible preliminary evaluation involving six development tasks, 15 artifacts, and three deployments. Conventional retrieval exposes 16 incompatible artifact occurrences as actionable, whereas CASE excludes them while retaining all applicable artifacts under the prototype's reference rules. Recency filtering reduces valid recall to 0.43, while removing feature-flag evidence from CASE reduces it to 0.13. Although these results demonstrate feasibility rather than improvements in coding-agent correctness, they reveal limitations of relevance-based retrieval and motivate a new research direction: enabling coding agents to reason not only about which information is relevant, but also whether it remains operationally valid.

### 9. Write Once, Instrument Anywhere: Portable, Sandboxed Tooling with WebAssembly Components

- arXiv ID: `2610.38682v1`
- Authors: Elizabeth Gilbert, Chris Fallin, Heather Miller, Ben L. Titzer
- Published: 2026-10-28
- Categories: `cs.SE`, `cs.PL`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38682v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38682v1)

Monitoring and debugging deployed software is crucial for scalability, performance, and robustness. Cross-cutting concerns are pervasive across software ecosystems, yet engineers must address them with a fragmented toolbox. While high-level frameworks exist to offer application-level types and semantics, they often lock a solution into specific languages, runtimes, or hardware architectures. Portability often forces engineers to choose lower-level instrumentation mechanisms. For example, in-language frameworks can trace a request across processes, but multi-language contexts require instrumentation at the boundary, for example using eBPF. While these frameworks provide powerful capabilities, they often result in 1) a tradeoff between application-level types or low-level bytes and packets, 2) non-composable domain-specific tools, 3) feature fragmentation across frameworks, and 4) tools that often run with unrestricted access to the program and its environment.
  We address these problems with Splicer, a WebAssembly component interposition framework that 1) provides semantically rich application-level types, 2) enables pluggable tooling with powerful capabilities, 3) reduces ecosystem fragmentation through porting across languages and hardware, and 4) confines each tool to a sandbox with explicitly granted permissions. We evaluate Splicer on a suite of benchmarks demonstrating the pluggability of non-trivial tooling written in multiple source languages and their performance.

### 10. DIFUZZI: A Differential Fuzzing Tool for Code Functional Similarity Assessment

- arXiv ID: `2610.38337v1`
- Authors: Simantika Bhattacharjee Dristi, Matthew B. Dwyer
- Published: 2026-10-28
- Categories: `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38337v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.38337v1)

A range of software engineering tasks, such as optimization and refactoring, seek to preserve the behavior of software components and require methods to assess functional similarity. Research has explored formal approaches to prove functional equivalence and used machine learning methods to approximate it, but the former does not scale and does not quantify the extent of similarity and the latter struggles when subtle semantic differences are present. Differential fuzzing offers a scalable approach for differencing programs semantically, but applying it often requires substantial manual effort to construct input generators that are well-suited to a target pair of programs. To address this challenge, we introduce DIFUZZI, which incorporates an LLM-based generator synthesis step that is iteratively validated and repaired. By automating this step, DIFUZZI paves the way for broader applicability of differential fuzzing. We release DIFUZZI at https://github.com/Dristi123/difuzzi_tool, with a demonstration video at https://youtu.be/FlHNOHuo9WE

### 11. ICALL-BENCH: Benchmarking LLM Interprocedural Reasoning in Binary Programs

- arXiv ID: `2610.37871v1`
- Authors: Shenglong Yao, Zirui Liu, Xiangchi Yuan, Wenke Lee
- Published: 2026-10-27
- Categories: `cs.SE`, `cs.CR`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37871v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37871v1)

Recent benchmarks show that LLMs achieve strong performance in both function-level binary understanding and end-to-end binary reverse engineering tasks, but whether LLMs can reason over interprocedural semantics in compiled binaries remains unclear. Function-level benchmarks offer limited visibility into cross-function relationships, while whole-program benchmarks typically evaluate final outcomes of tasks that may be solvable from local clues alone, making it difficult to disentangle interprocedural reasoning from exploration, tool use, execution, and retrieval. To address this limitation, we introduce ICALL-BENCH, a benchmark that directly evaluates whether LLMs and agents can understand and reason over interprocedural semantics in binary programs, using indirect-call target recovery as a measurable probe. ICALL-BENCH covers 50 complete real-world C/C++ programs spanning different complexity levels and multiple optimization configurations, with an average of 74,746 lines of code and 941 functions per program. We further introduce three analysis regimes spanning model-only reasoning, static tool-augmented agents, and Internet-enabled open-world agents to examine how intrinsic reasoning, active information acquisition, and external knowledge each contribute to interprocedural binary analysis. We evaluate five recent LLMs and their corresponding agents. Our results reveal a wide separation across current models and agents, with program-level verifiable success rates ranging from 23% to 81%. They also show that greater information access does not consistently lead to better results, with particularly limited gains on the hardest cases, where deeper interprocedural reasoning remains the primary bottleneck. Uneven performance across models and frequent verifiable failures even under an optimistic evaluation indicate that robust binary interprocedural reasoning remains an open challenge.
