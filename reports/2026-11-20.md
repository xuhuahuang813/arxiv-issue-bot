### 1. Bridging the Discovery Gap in Solid Dataspaces with a Semantic Data Catalog

- arXiv ID: `2611.26611v1`
- Authors: Florian Hölken, Alexander Paulus, Tobias Meisen, André Pomp
- Published: 2026-11-19
- Categories: `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26611v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26611v1)

Dataspaces are an emerging concept to enable secure and controlled data sharing between multiple stakeholders. However, most existing dataspace architectures, such as those based on the concepts of IDSA (International Data Spaces Association) and Gaia-X, are heavily concept-driven and require significant effort to implement, making it difficult to quickly build a functional dataspace. Solid (Social Linked Data) offers a compelling alternative by providing a lighter and more practical approach. However, for Solid to function as a true dataspace, like traditional dataspaces, it must be built in a decentralized manner, i.e., the dataspace must consist of more than a single Solid server. However, this decentralization introduces a key challenge: Data Discovery. Unlike traditional dataspace architectures, which often include predefined components such as data catalogs to facilitate data discovery and access, Solid does not inherently provide such mechanisms. As a result, managing and locating relevant data across multiple Solid servers and pods becomes a significant barrier to usability. To address this gap, we present a FAIR-compliant data catalog for Solid. Our catalog follows the FAIR principles (Findability, Accessibility, Interoperability, and Reusability) by integrating semantic models that describe all available data sources within the Solid ecosystem. This approach enables enhanced data discovery, improves accessibility through standardized metadata, enhances interoperability between different data sources, and promotes data reuse across applications. By bridging this critical gap, our catalog improves the usability of Solid-based dataspaces, making them a viable option for decentralized and open data ecosystems, such as smart city applications.

### 2. CRAFT: Constrained Route planning with Adaptive Framework and Template-based code generation

- arXiv ID: `2611.25128v1`
- Authors: Shahd Elmahallawy, Ahmed Eldawy
- Published: 2026-11-18
- Categories: `cs.DB`, `cs.AI`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25128v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25128v1)

Everyday routing requests are rarely a point-to-point shortest path. A driver may need to visit stops, sometimes in a fixed order, under time budgets or distance limits, and perhaps recharge their EV car along the way. Each combination is a distinct routing problem that classically requires its own algorithm, so no fixed solver covers the space of queries users may ask. This paper presents CRAFT, which takes a constrained routing query as free text and returns a route that satisfies the user's requirements on a real road network. An LLM parses the query and generates four search functions, which a generic graph-search engine executes on the road network. We formalize the underlying problem as State-Expanded RCSP (SE-RCSP), which extends resource-constrained shortest paths (RCSP) with non-additive state such as node-dependent resets and discrete progress tracking. We evaluate CRAFT on 2,600 natural-language queries over California road networks against two baselines, analyzing correctness, constraint violations, long-distance scalability, and semantic POI filtering.

### 3. From Industrial Product Documentation to Automated Digital Product Passport Generation: An Experimental Study of Large Language Models

- arXiv ID: `2611.24998v1`
- Authors: Marco Buecheler, Grant Richards, Nathan Hartman
- Published: 2026-11-18
- Categories: `cs.SE`, `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24998v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24998v1)

Manufacturing companies that are planning to sell certain products in the European Union (EU) will be required to provide mandatory digital product passports (DPPs). The use of DPPs is to provide standardized product information throughout multiple product life cycles in a circular economy. Required passport information, such as technical specifications, material composition, and environmental product footprint, is available in manufacturers' data sources but is scattered across different heterogeneous systems, documents, and data formats. The given paper investigates using large language models (LLMs) to automate the generation of DPPs based on the asset administration shell (AAS). A retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) approach is used to extract product information from industrial documentation and generate populated AAS instances. Product documentation is processed and divided into text chunks. The text chunks are then stored in a persistent ChromaDB vector database using vector embeddings. The retrieval process combines semantic and lexical search to identify relevant information based on standardized AAS submodel template requirements. The retrieved information is provided to five LLMs to generate structured AAS instances. The experiments cover different industrial products and AAS submodels. Four levels of prompt guidance are investigated to assess their influence on generation quality. The generated passports are evaluated against a ground truth. The experimental evaluation includes 4,560 executions across 76 product-submodel combinations. Of the generated passport outputs, 97.4% are valid JSON, while 44.9% pass the full AAS validation procedure. Under deterministic scoring, mean completeness, conditional accuracy, and overall field correctness are 49.6%, 58.3%, and 30.8%, respectively. An LLM-as-a-judge yields better scores of 66.5%, 88.9%, and 60.4% for the same outputs.

### 4. Backtracking Agent Applications Security Incidents

- arXiv ID: `2611.26525v1`
- Authors: Yicheng Cai, Taegyu Kim, Peng Liu
- Published: 2026-11-19
- Categories: `cs.CR`, `cs.AI`, `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26525v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26525v1)

Agent applications are increasingly deployed, yet agent-specific attacks pose real threats to them. Upon a security incident, organizations backtrack to identify the CEP (compromise entry point), remediate the damage, and prevent its recurrence. The main artifacts available for backtracking are trajectory logs, which record agent functionalities, and source code; app(lication)-specific function call logs are often fairly limited. However, trajectory logs are at the agent functionality abstraction layer, while the CEP candidates are function calls at the app code execution layer. Due to this semantic gap, the analyst needs to manually inspect the relevant code for each CEP candidate to infer its corresponding agent functionality. In this work, we present Origin, a backtracking tool for the analyst to submit a small number of queries to identify CEPs that existing tools cannot provide. Our approach poses two challenges: (1) define the intermediate abstraction that captures the mapping relationships between the two semantic layers; (2) determine the region-to-variable mapping for constructing the cross-layer graph defined in the intermediate abstraction. Each region is a specific group of agent functionalities. Origin addresses the challenges by (1) the cross-layer dependency graph (CLDG) that connects region-inferred functions at the semantic level to the variables in the low-level taint propagation subgraph, plus the associated query language, and (2) the region inference algorithm. Case studies on five popular agent applications across eight attack incidents show that an analyst using Origin correctly identifies all the CEPs.

### 5. Auto-Alpha: A Framework for Autonomous Factor Research

- arXiv ID: `2611.25732v1`
- Authors: Hui Cai, Kangcheng Deng, Tianyi Ma, Qixuan Yang, Jiacheng Lu, Rui Sun, Beidi Luan, Jing Li, Zuo Bai
- Published: 2026-11-19
- Categories: `q-fin.CP`, `cs.AI`, `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25732v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25732v1)

Autonomous factor research requires generating economic hypotheses as well as implementing and testing them. We present Auto-Alpha, a framework that connects report-guided initialization to self-directed research through a test-driven implementation loop. An idea agent proposes economic mechanisms and signal constructions; a research agent develops them with backtest feedback. Semantic audit, quantitative review, and human admission select factors for a persistent library, while an Experience Library retains research insights. Starting from 120 research reports, Auto-Alpha builds an initial library of 32 factors. It then generates new ideas and refines existing candidates, producing 420 implementations and retaining 62 additional factors. We evaluate factor quality and portfolio performance using a fixed library snapshot of 105 factors. The median 1-day Rank IC is 0.0301, and a weekly Top-300 portfolio returns 16.81% annualized after stated costs over 2020-2026, versus 1.71% for the CSI 1000 price index. These retrospective results document an operating research process and its artifacts; controlled evaluation is needed to isolate the contribution of its components.
