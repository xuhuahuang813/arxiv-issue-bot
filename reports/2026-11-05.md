### 1. A Comparative Study of Importance Attribution to Columns in Deep Learning over Relational Databases

- arXiv ID: `2611.05986v1`
- Authors: Hila Levi-Yosefi, Shunit Agmon, Ester Livshits, Agapi Rissaki, Benny Kimelfeld
- Published: 2026-11-04
- Categories: `cs.LG`, `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05986v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05986v1)

Deep learning over relational databases has led to increasingly effective prediction models, motivating a growing need to explain their behavior. We study explanation methods that assign importance scores to individual database attributes, focusing on a fundamental question: to what extent does the choice of importance-attribution method matter? We develop a methodology to systematically compare importance-attribution methods and analyze their similarities and differences. In particular, we study two aspects of the explanations: which attributes they deem important, captured by differences in attribute rankings, and how importance scores vary across attributes, captured by differences in score distributions. We further investigate whether these differences are reflected in the conventional measure of explanation quality, fidelity.
  We apply our methodology in an extensive empirical study of four methods: Monte Carlo Shapley, Kernel SHAP, single-attribute impact, and mask learning. We experiment with multiple datasets and tasks using two RDL architectures: heterogeneous GraphSAGE and Relational Graph Transformers. Our experiments show that explanation methods behave differently depending on the prediction type: regression or classification. In regression, most methods exhibit high agreement in attribute rankings, whereas classification yields considerably less consensus. Notably, while mask learning frequently diverges in its importance distributions, Shapley-based methods and single-attribute impact often converge to similar explanations. This suggests that, in many settings, single-attribute impact can replace Shapley at substantially lower computational cost. Overall, our study provides methodological tools for comparing importance-attribution methods, and empirical evidence that their choice has important consequences for interpreting relational deep learning models.

### 2. When Is One Query per Template Enough? Adapting Learned Cost Models to New Hardware

- arXiv ID: `2611.05892v1`
- Authors: Krish Patel
- Published: 2026-11-04
- Categories: `cs.DB`, `cs.LG`, `cs.PF`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05892v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05892v1)

A learned cost model that predicts query runtimes is trained on measurements from one machine. When the database moves to other hardware, its plans stay the same but their runtimes change, each by its own factor. We ask how to adapt such a model from k queries measured on the new machine: which queries to run, how to use their runtimes, and how many are enough. On PostgreSQL we timed the same plans of 1,162 TPC-H and TPC-DS queries in six environments: a laptop and a server, each with all its processors or pinned to one, the server also pinned to two, and the laptop also with a small buffer pool. No single factor describes the slowdown between two environments: most of its variance lies between query templates, so a speed factor stops improving after a few queries. A correction that fits separate lines for serial and parallel plans and adds an offset for every measured template brings the moved model, with one measured query per template, to an error (geometric mean over templates of the median Q-error) of 1.10 on TPC-H and 1.19 on TPC-DS, close to the same correction on a model trained on the new machine; fine-tuning a DACE-style neural cost model on the same queries gains at most 2%. On templates never measured, no correction we tried is on average more than 2% better than the speed factor. We then fixed hypotheses and timed new data. On TPC-H at ten times the scale the errors follow these results, but two of five repeated tests miss. On the Join Order Benchmark, whose queries differ widely within a template, the model errs by 2.36 even when retrained on the new machine, no correction gains much, and only the test on new templates holds; boosted trees and a neural model fit these queries better, and correcting the neural model works as well as fine-tuning it. We report every test as registered.

### 3. Optimizing Geo-replicated Deterministic Databases

- arXiv ID: `2611.05882v1`
- Authors: Yunhao Mao, Shiquan Zhang, Gengrui Zhang, Hans-Arno Jacobsen
- Published: 2026-11-04
- Categories: `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05882v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05882v1)

In transactional databases, achieving active-active replication among geo-distributed replicas is challenging due to the high coordination cost in ensuring the serializability of concurrent cross-node transactions. Deterministic concurrency control (DCC) has emerged as a promising alternative: it establishes a global order for a batch of transactions before execution and thereby amortizes this coordination cost across the batch. To better understand the performance characteristics of DCC, we conduct a theoretical analysis of traditional two-phase locking and optimistic concurrency control to account for geo-replication and introduce a model for DCC. We compare the three paradigms and identify the conditions under which DCC is advantageous. Guided by the insights from this analysis, we then present Quirinus, a DCC-based geo-replicated database that optimizes transaction execution through speculative execution, transaction reordering, integration of conflict-free replicated data types (CRDTs), and fast-path commit. Even without these optimizations, Quirinus achieves up to 6.2x the TPC-C throughput of Tiga under our configuration.

### 4. Whiel: A Neurosymbolic Platform for Certified Relational Program Verification

- arXiv ID: `2611.05612v1`
- Authors: Jesse Comer, Fangzhu Shen, Shuo Zhang, Mayur Naik, Sudeepa Roy, Val Tannen
- Published: 2026-11-03
- Categories: `cs.DB`, `cs.AI`, `cs.LO`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05612v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05612v1)

We present the Whiel (a contraction of Whi(le-R)el) system for Lean-certified reasoning about imperative relational programs. The system can encode containment and equivalence problems for Datalog, its extensions with negation, and the core fragment of graph query languages. It can also represent optimized or incremental evaluation algorithms that are not expressible in the original query language, as well as first-order specifications of integrity constraints, schema mappings, and views arising in data integration, exchange, and sharing. Whiel's program states are database instances, and its imperative structure enables compositional Hoare-style reasoning with first-order relational specifications, using prophecy variables and leveled invariants to support inductive arguments. Whiel supports neurosymbolic discovery of loop invariants whose verification conditions can be checked using the Vampire first-order theorem prover. Counterexamples are fed back into the neurosymbolic pipeline, while proposed solutions are translated into Lean proof certificates. To validate the Whiel system, we evaluate its performance on a curated benchmark suite. In summary, we envision the Whiel system as a tool for tackling mathematically demanding, typically undecidable problems involving complex query and constraint languages, with a high degree of trust in its output.

### 5. Non-redundant Independence Atoms

- arXiv ID: `2611.05568v1`
- Authors: Attila Sali, Jun Yan
- Published: 2026-11-03
- Categories: `math.CO`, `cs.DB`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05568v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05568v1)

Independence atom is a key concept in relational database theory. In the present paper, we prove that the maximum possible size of a non-redundant set of independence atoms on $n$ attributes is asymptotically $(1+o(1))\binom{n}{\floor{n/2}}$. It turns out that this problem is closely related to two extremal set theory problems, the Katona-Tarján question on ``V''-free families of subsets of $[n]$ for the lower bound, and Kleitman's bound on union-free families for the upper bound.

### 6. AgentTrustCI: Build-Time Policy, Grounding, and Trajectory Tests for Java LLM Agents

- arXiv ID: `2611.06674v1`
- Authors: Sachin Gupta
- Published: 2026-11-04
- Categories: `cs.SE`, `cs.AI`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06674v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06674v1)

Infrastructure security does not establish whether an LLM agent selected an allowed tool, grounded its answer in tool output, or followed a required workflow. AgentTrustCI is a Java/JUnit 5 framework that expresses these application-layer expectations as policy, grounding, and trajectory checks over recorded traces. A deterministic replay implementation supports repeatable build-time tests; a judge interface provides an extension point for optional semantic evaluation. On 55 synthetic cases spanning seven enterprise domains, the full pipeline correctly classifies all 30 injected faults and 25 passing controls, including ten non-trivial controls, and misses none of 12 designated critical cases. Each single-layer configuration correctly classifies 35 cases because the corpus deliberately isolates ten faults per layer. A grounded answer obtained through a disallowed sequence illustrates the additional coverage of trajectory checks. Repeated measurements quantify validator execution separately from process startup and reporting. These results establish coverage of the authored regression suite, not production accuracy. We describe a production-trace validation protocol, optional LLM-judge integration, and maintenance of expectations as workflows evolve. The contribution is a practical pattern for expressing behavioral contracts within an existing Java release pipeline.

### 7. Scythe: Semantic-Aware Rewriting for Fast,Effective Program Reduction

- arXiv ID: `2611.06515v1`
- Authors: Ioanna Moraiti, Charalambos Mitropoulos, Artem Anchikov, Sotirios Ioannidis, Dimitris Mitropoulos, Thodoris Sotiropoulos
- Published: 2026-11-04
- Categories: `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06515v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06515v1)

Automated program reduction shrinks a program into a smaller one that preserves a property of interest. Language-agnostic grammar-based reducers are easy to retarget across languages, but because they reason only about syntax, they cannot perform the coupled edits needed to remove whole declarations, e.g., a method cannot be deleted while it is still called. As a result, they waste oracle calls on edits that cannot succeed and often leave irrelevant declarations behind. We present SCYTHE, a fast, semantic-aware pre-processing step that runs in front of any grammar-based reducer without modifying it. SCYTHE encodes the program as a typed declaration dependency graph and applies a set of semantic-aware operators that decouple declarations from their use sites, making them removable while keeping the program well-typed. Fine-grained token-level minimization is then left to the downstream reducer. On 73 benchmarks across C, Java, and Solidity, combining SCYTHE with PERSES never yields a larger result than PERSES alone, and a strictly smaller one on 70 of 73 cases. On average, the combination produces programs 55.6% smaller (up to 95.5%) and is 2.3$\times$ faster (up to 7.4$\times$). SCYTHE also boosts LATRA, a reducer that, unlike the purely syntactic PERSES, also applies language-specific transformations: the combination produces programs 10.6% smaller (up to 61.8%) and is 2.4$\times$ faster on average. ### 8. Reassessing Dataset Quality and Fine-Tuning Practices for Vulnerability Detection on PrimeVul dataset

- arXiv ID: `2611.06373v1`
- Authors: Norbert Sándor Szolnoki, Gábor Antal
- Published: 2026-11-04
- Categories: `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06373v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06373v1)

Recent studies report promising results from supervised fine-tuning of pre-trained code language models for automated vulnerability detection on benchmarks such as PrimeVul. In this paper, we present a large-scale empirical evaluation of both model performance and dataset quality in this setting.
  We systematically fine-tune three widely used encoder models (CodeBERT, GraphCodeBERT, and UniXCoder) across five imbalance mitigation strategies, extensive hyperparameter sweeps, and multiple random seeds, resulting in 770 independent training runs.
  Our results reveal a consistent pattern. While standard metrics such as F1 and ROC-AUC occasionally suggest improvements, security-critical and calibration-aware measures tell a different story. Matthews Correlation Coefficient remains near zero, predicted probabilities are poorly calibrated, and evaluation under strict false-positive constraints reveals high false-negative rates. Neither imbalance handling, threshold optimization, nor post-hoc calibration recovers a meaningful discriminative signal.
  We also conduct a data quality assessment of the PrimeVul benchmark and identify structural issues such as minimal semantic differences between vulnerable and fixed functions, and annotation noise introduced by commit-level labeling.
  These findings suggests that the PrimeVul dataset, in its current form, presents significant challenges for reliable AI-based vulnerability detection in software engineering with the methodologies we used. Our study highlights the need for more robust dataset design, improved evaluation protocols, and rethinking of fine-tuning practices for security-critical tasks.

### 9. Bug2Scenario: Root-Cause-Preserving Test Scenario Amplification for Autonomous Driving Software

- arXiv ID: `2611.06229v1`
- Authors: Yuchen Cao, Zhenyu Mao, Jacky Wai Keung, Yifei Wang, Yihan Liao, Jingyu Zhang
- Published: 2026-11-04
- Categories: `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06229v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06229v1)

Simulation-based fuzzing exposes autonomous driving system (ADS) failures by repeatedly mutating and executing scenarios, but converting one failure into reusable regression scenarios remains difficult. Such variants test whether the mechanism persists after software changes, for example by varying weather or actor placement while retaining a collision's actor-route conflict. We present Bug2Scenario, a root-cause-guided post-fuzzing framework. From scenario data, traces, and oracle evidence, it extracts a root-cause pattern. An LLM proposes semantic variations over actors, missions, routes, and weather. Deterministic rules construct DriveFuzz-compatible scenarios. Execution evidence and manual review assess preservation. Separating these responsibilities prevents fluency, syntax, or recurring labels from masquerading as causal evidence. Under matched seeds, six LLM backbones, candidate budget, and execution pipeline, Bug2Scenario improves strict preservation by 6.7 percentage points (p=0.104; not significant) and boundary-inclusive preservation by 10.6 points (p=0.011). Feedback helps some initially unsuccessful seeds yield a strictly preserved candidate. Seed-level stopping then avoids further API calls and simulator runs. Remaining failures reflect actor-placement constraints, simulator instability, and incomplete or conflicting causal evidence. Bug2Scenario thus produces reliable ADS regression assets. Our implementation is available at https://github.com/YgcCoder/Bug2Sce.

### 10. A Hybrid Approach for Automated Security Pattern Recovery

- arXiv ID: `2611.05823v1`
- Authors: Anh Minh Nguyen, Tuan Dat Tran, Alzubair Hassan, Liliana Pasquale
- Published: 2026-11-04
- Categories: `cs.CR`, `cs.AI`, `cs.SE`
- Links: [abs](http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05823v1) | [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05823v1)

Security-by-design emphasises integrating security into software design and development. Security pattern catalogues operationalise this principle by prescribing reusable architectural solutions to recurring security problems. However, recovering their implementations from source code remains challenging because security patterns are abstract, span multiple components, and admit many valid implementations. We present SecPatRec, a hybrid approach for automated security pattern recovery from source code repositories. We treat a security pattern as a reference architecture and frame its recovery as a deductive task: determining which prescribed roles are present, grounding them in code entities, and recovering their interaction sequences. SecPatRec pairs a ReAct-style LLM agent equipped with semantic and lexical code search for role detection and grounding with static code property graph analysis for interaction recovery. We curate a human-validated ground-truth dataset of 100 pattern instances covering five security patterns across 93 open-source Java and Python repositories, and introduce a three-level evaluation framework measuring recovery correctness against this ground truth. We evaluate SecPatRec using four LLMs, including proprietary and open-source models. Averaged over all models and pattern instances, SecPatRec achieves role-detection accuracy of 0.84, role-grounding similarity of 0.84, and an interaction-recovery score of 0.66. Compared to a one-shot end-to-end LLM baseline, SecPatRec achieves higher aggregate recovery scores and lower estimated LLM API costs per run, while processing repositories that exceed the baseline's configured input limit. The recovered role mappings and statically derived interaction sequences provide structural and behavioural evidence that can support inspection of security-pattern implementations.
